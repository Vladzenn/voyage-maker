# Fae Appearance and Variation Guide

Use alongside Character Creation Format and Portrait Creation Format. The authoritative lore is **Fae — Bodies and Individual Variation** in the active world. This is an authoring aid, not a division of fae into fixed subraces, a native trait pack or a completed character-creation screen.

Fae are the setting’s most visually varied people. Animal parts are characteristic, but their arrangement belongs to an individual. Most are shorter than humans, most ears are pointed and the common appearance is a winged humanoid. Satyr features, antlers, hooves, wooden scales and bird or insect features are equally valid individual designs. Relatives need not match exactly. A short adult has a mature face and adult proportions; height never establishes childhood. Do not infer a common lifespan, conversion ratio, disposition or power from a body feature.

## A repeatable random draw

Record each draw before writing the appearance. Preserve a declared feature rather than rolling over the author’s concept. These weights are practical authoring defaults, not biological census figures.

| Decision | Draw out of 100 | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Stature | 0–59 | Short adult; this batch used 85–139 cm |
| Stature | 60–91 | Around human height; this batch used 150–194 cm |
| Stature | 92–99 | Very tall adult; this batch used 214–244 cm |
| Starting silhouette | 0–64 | Winged humanoid; draw uniformly among moth, butterfly, dragonfly and feathered bird wings |
| Starting silhouette | 65–99 | Draw a coherent starting combination: ram horns and hoofed legs; antlers and hoofed legs; or wooden scale patches and a feathered tail |
| Ears | 0–84 | Pointed |
| Ears | 85–99 | Rounded |
| Additional detail | 0–24 | Draw one compatible extra: feather cuffs, a short deer tail or fine wooden scale patches |
| Additional detail | 25–99 | No extra feature needed |

The starting combinations keep an initial draw readable. They do not restrict the species to those combinations. Check anatomy and avoid adding redundant tails, unrelated ears, several conflicting wing pairs or an unreadable pile of features. If a draw creates a real conflict, document the conflict and correct eligibility before redrawing; do not reroll merely to get a favorite design. Specify the individual’s actual wing count, wing attachment, ears, limbs, hands, feet and tail where applicable.

Affinity count is a separate independent draw under the existing 65% / 25% / 10% rule. Appearance does not assign an affinity. Nature is not mandatory for a fae. Flight, shrinking, carrying capacity and unusual senses require the individual’s actual anatomy and authored training; none is a free power awarded by a visual roll.

## Cards and portraits

Put height, adult presentation, hair and eye color and the selected features in the existing Appearance paragraph. Carry the key appearance facts into native basicInfo and visualDescription so generation has explicit individual facts to follow. The species label Fae is not sufficient direction by itself. Preserve those facts across subsequent updates.

Keep the standard card headings and equipment paragraph. Small people can have adult jobs, families, romances and field competence. Buildings, tools and clothes can accommodate size differences without every conversation becoming a height joke. Wings do not mean someone likes heights. A wingless satyr must not acquire wings merely because the renderer recognizes the word fae.

For future character-creation design, this provides choices to expose deliberately: stature, ears, wing form or wingless body, horns/antlers, lower-limb form, wooden scale patches and tail/feather details. The population update does **not** change the current player-creation trait selection or grant mechanical bonuses for these appearance choices.
