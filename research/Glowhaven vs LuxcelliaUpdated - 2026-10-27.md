# Glowhaven compared with LuxcelliaUpdated

Date: 2026-10-27

## Scope and evidence

Read-only analysis of the updated reference at C:/Users/VladCodes/Downloads/LuxcelliaUpdated.json and the active WorldAlpha files. Both full exports identify as heroesVersion 37. The current full world matches the standalone NPC and location sections. The standalone lore has the same keyed entry content; serialization/property order differs. No canon, portraits, placements, mechanics, or world files were changed for this comparison.

Methods: section inventory; counts and image coverage; exact-key checks of NPC locations, areas and factions plus location regions and internal area paths; inspection of selected NPCs, locations, lore, starts, quest chains, completion triggers, clock scripts, and AI task instructions. This is a structural and editorial review, not a full schema validation, exhaustive prose review, or gameplay test. A large authored catalog does not establish reliability or fun.

## Overall assessment

Luxcellia is substantially more complete as an authored RPG. Its strongest advantage is the number of connections between a character's problem, a location's secret, a quest, a reward, and persistent consequences. Glowhaven has a strong, concentrated cast and a substantial map, but much of its playable activity still depends on the narrator improvising the connections.

Glowhaven does not need to match a continent-sized reference's counts. Its city-and-four-academy scope supports a denser recurring social world. More characters and more map areas are not the most urgent needs.

## What Luxcellia does particularly well

### 1. It gives places functions and concealed causes

The Silver Crescent Academy entry joins public social structure, named authority figures, the practical campus, and an underlying magical mystery. Its hiddenInfo gives separate people different fragments of knowledge, making investigation possible through more than one contact. It does not merely announce a secret and resolve it.

The Adventurer's Guild Central Branch describes actual service flows: registrations, appraisal, salvage, meals, training, accommodation, and disputes. Named NPCs occupy those services. Its mess hall has a reason to return beyond buying food.

Floor Zero Gatehouse joins admissions, equipment, medical checks, training, cohorts, and dangerous descents. Its hiddenInfo adds imperfect wards and competing institutional interests. The institution can generate work, relationships, mistakes, and consequences.

Glowhaven's map mostly supplies room identity, a short description, and paths. That is a good navigation base. It rarely supplies an immediate situation, service restriction, political pressure, or change that makes the player act differently.

Suggested adaptation: enrich a small set of existing hubs first. State what the player can do there, who handles it, what is happening, what is concealed if anything, who knows, and what can change. Quiet rooms do not all need conspiracies. Do not copy Luxcellia's ancient conduit explanation into the Evertree's lore.

### 2. NPC traits lead to repeatable actions and escalating trust

Aria Hoshikaze's pride affects speech, preparation, rivalry, and the way she admits respect. Her family problem connects to other people and evidence that can be used in conflicting ways. Koji Inuzuka's cooking is a service, a way to read his mood, a grief ritual, and a potential expedition connection.

The reusable method is observable behavior plus meaningful stakes, not maximum biography length. Glowhaven already has strong Core, Speaking Style, Courtship Style, Jealousy Style, pressure points, and knowledge boundaries. It does not need to replace that format.

Suggested adaptation: author specific situations for selected cast members and a few observable changes in trust. These can live in quest or encounter design rather than new mandatory character-card headings. Ranka Kagari should remain careless and self-interested when the situation tests her; Issei Kurose does not need a redemption secret to create good play. Different players should be able to help, oppose, exploit, or avoid a character.

### 3. Starts promise a recognizable kind of play

Luxcellia's scholarship start establishes social pressure, named peers, a focus, and starting money. The gatehouse start establishes accommodation, a job prospect, paperwork, medical concerns, and a choice about joining a descent. Each provides an immediate reason to engage with its setting.

Glowhaven currently has Random and Phoenix Wing: Arrival Court. The default is a flexible, low-stakes greeting. That supports freedom, but it only lightly introduces the specific appeal of the four academies or magical city.

Suggested adaptation: one purposeful opening per academy with a small problem and a choice, while retaining Random for open backgrounds. A purpose can be a practical exercise, a disputed booking, a lost focus, or a supervised task; it need not be an attack. Enrollment should follow the opening the player chose rather than be imposed on every background.

### 4. Character creation connects to equipment and progression

The reference's Bow trait connects an identity to Archery and an actual starting bow. Its ability requirements refer to skills and traits. Its items have defined categories and effects. Skill descriptions explain boundaries and give difficulty examples.

Glowhaven has eight skills, no trait categories, no trait entries, no authored item types, and no authored player ability entries. Its progression configuration grants ability points and periodic trait picks, but no authored level-up trait pool is populated. The engine can still improvise content; the gap is the absence of a curated player build and advancement offering.

Suggested adaptation: a modest foundation of backgrounds/species where needed, affinities, casting styles, starter equipment, and spells that produce different choices. Preserve the established rule that every school of magic is learnable. An affinity should assist learning rather than become an exclusive class lock. Do not turn every NPC's narrative technique into a global player ability entry.

### 5. It connects jobs, prices, supplies, and rewards

Currency lore, coin items, profession skills, inventory guidance, and contract reward instructions reinforce each other. Loot names and rewards are given common conventions. This reduces the need for every shop or quest to invent a new economic scale.

Glowhaven already establishes farms, fisheries, enchanted exports, boutiques, shops, and working residents. It has not authored a common currency, starter inventory, item catalog, or deliberate rewards economy.

Suggested adaptation: choose a simple money scale and a short useful goods list. Define what a meal, a night's lodging, a basic repair, a potion, and beginner work mean relative to one another. Make the city's enchanted-item trade visible through playable repair, delivery, inspection, and crafting problems. Detailed accounting is unnecessary.

### 6. It tells the narrator how to use its roster

Luxcellia has explicit roster-first instructions, role matching, named opening cast, and instructions that scene presence does not imply recruitment. Its movement guidance preserves reliable home anchors and gives travelers reasons to appear.

This directly addresses Glowhaven's intended experience: many possible acquaintances, only a few close companions. We should prioritize existing staff, merchants, and classmates when their established roles fit, introduce manageable groups, and keep local contacts at their duties when the player leaves. Our narrator-driven social scenes should remain compatible with our existing combat-only NPC-intent instructions; copying Luxcellia's social-intent architecture wholesale would conflict with that choice.

### 7. Time supports availability

The reference has actual clock_plan, clock_state, and clock_map scripts. Its planning script estimates elapsed time from actions and explicit durations, maintains stored hour/day values, updates day/time-of-day state, and supplies a clock instruction to narration. Written guidance then connects time to light, meals, business hours, and NPC routines.

This is more substantial than a sentence telling the narrator to remember time. However, there are no npc-placement effects in the inspected general trigger effects or scripts. Its clock should not be described as proof of a complete mechanical timetable that relocates every NPC.

Suggested adaptation: start with broad shared day phases and individual routine guidance. Use explicit relocation only when dependable presence is important. Respect travel, conversations, companions, combat, injuries, and appointments. Test skipped time and mixed actions before adopting any parsing script.

## Where Glowhaven already has advantages

1. Concentrated recurring cast. Four neighboring institutions make rivalries, friendships, and cross-school interactions easier to revisit than a continent-wide cast.
2. Explicit relationships. An exact full-name scan of basicInfo, hiddenInfo and personality found another roster character named in 82/128 Glowhaven cards, versus 127/372 reference cards. This is a narrow text measure, not a quality score, but shows our roster is not merely isolated biographies. Of our 59 zero-level city additions, 20 name another roster NPC in those fields.
3. Public/private separation. Our cards keep sensitive motives and knowledge boundaries in hiddenInfo. The reference includes public Romance Archetype text in 355/372 NPC basicInfo fields. That is not a requirement to adopt, especially given the user's dislike of exposed card analysis.
4. Useful adult power separation. Students are level 15, professors 50, headmasters 70, Archons 85, and Ilyra Stillglass 100 in the current data. These are deliberate distinctions rather than forgotten power metadata.
5. Modes already have genuine design work. The 12 modes cover different loops and preserve player choices, lore, continuity, and actual outcomes. Their count is not a major gap.
6. Smaller scope. A single city can have richer consequences and more familiar faces without reproducing 28 regions.
7. Understandable scale. More prose is not automatically better. Glowhaven can preserve concise cards and clear instructions while adding the connections that support play.

## Concrete findings in the current Glowhaven export

### Four unresolved faction references

The City Guard exists in lore and four NPC faction fields but has no faction entry in the current seven-entry faction map:
- Nao Tsukuda
- Daisuke Mochida
- Ayaka Naruse
- Tetsuya Ōmi

Add the intended faction record or deliberately change the references. Do not invent a second differently named organization as a workaround.

### Fifty-nine city NPCs have level 0

All 59 added city NPCs currently store level 0. Some are ordinary residents, but the group includes trained guards. Give combat-capable residents intentional levels appropriate to their roles. This is not a reason to give all shopkeepers professor-level combat power.

### Wraith Island Prison descriptions conflict

The location basicInfo says its condition and access are unspecified. The World Lore entry establishes an operational remote high-security prison, and the cast includes people with authorized prison-related work. These descriptions should agree on the prison being operational while retaining its discovery restrictions. Unknown to the player does not mean undecided by the author.

### Production notes have returned to exported lore

The current export includes wording such as "reserved for later development" in Diplomacy and the Wider Kingdom and a not-yet-established division of duties in The Four Academies and Civic Service. The user explicitly asked for finished in-world prose without such commentary. Unsettled author decisions belong in draft notes; known lore entries should state established facts.

### The default opening and Hinae Komori's placement need a deliberate staging rule

The default start presents Hinae Komori at Arrival Court, while her current NPC placement is Lecture Hall. An opening can deliberately bring her to the court, but that should be expressed consistently rather than relying on narration to contradict saved placement. Preserve the user's distributed baseline roster.

### Narration currently suppresses descriptive atmosphere

aiInstructions.generateStory["How to Use the Narrator"] instructs stage-direction narration, suppresses most ambience and sensory description, and says "one noun is enough" in doubtful cases. Luxcellia's main voice asks for stylish light-novel narration and vivid concrete imagery.

This is a style choice, not an automatic defect. If the user wants Luxcellia-like scene texture, adding more location lore alone will not produce it while our narrator instructions actively suppress it. Revise the small relevant style rules with that goal, retaining short turns and player agency.

### Missing civic operating rules

The four academies' civic roles are a declared central concept but not assigned responsibilities. The Evertree's limits, access, oversight, and practical effects need enough definition to answer ordinary questions without either making Ilyra Stillglass solve every problem or making her inexplicably powerless. The Council needs enough membership, jurisdiction, and election procedure to create intelligible political choices.

### Minimal authored repeatable activity

The only authored quest, First Words, is an optional opening conversation with explicit completion through its one narrative event. It is a small technical/social demonstration. It does not yet represent a class, field task, competition, city commission, or personal relationship arc.

## What to learn cautiously from Luxcellia

- Its 380 blank quest completion queries are not an isolated failure: 380 separate party quest triggers each use accepted status and an actual-completion story condition to issue quest-progress, covering all 380 distinct quests. Copying only its quest map would omit essential dependencies.
- Arcs are empty in the reference. Named quest chains and conditions supply some sequencing. An arcs count is not a sensible quality score.
- Much of its huge trait map supports presentation and appearance rather than new systems.
- Its public romance labels conflict with the user's card-visibility preference.
- Its starts and some quest briefs deliberately prescribe strong premises, feelings, rival roles, and genre outcomes. Borrow their specificity without forcing those exact assumptions into Glowhaven's open backgrounds.
- Its extensive overlapping instructions create a large maintenance surface. Our approximate serialized AI section is 31,882 characters versus 308,930 in the reference. This does not imply every leaf is used on every turn or establish a usage cost. Compact, non-conflicting guidance is preferable to copying every repair rule.
- The clock parses language heuristically. Mixed actions, conditional statements, refusals, and unusual phrasing are practical test cases.
- Higher quantities and clean local references do not demonstrate that all quests and triggers work in a live game.

## Suggested development order

1. Correct the small consistency issues: City Guard, operational prison descriptions, baseline power metadata, opening cast placement, and authoring notes inside exported lore.
2. Finish a civic outline: academy duties, the Evertree's practical rules, the Council's working authority, and public responses to magical danger.
3. Make a small selection of existing places fully playable: all four academy campuses, a social/commercial hub, the colosseum, and one field destination. Add services, current pressures, knowledgeable NPCs, and discoverable details only where useful.
4. Define the minimum player setup: meaningful starting options, equipment, a compact spell/ability offering, a few skill boundaries, and the intended handling of mana, money, and training.
5. Author one small academy opening and a short activity arc per school, plus a few city jobs. Give them clear completion and persistent outcomes. No need to approach 100 starts or 380 quests.
6. Give selected important NPCs situations with alternative resolutions. Use the player's small chosen social circle; do not activate every student's personal arc at once.
7. Add light time/routine support and deliberate cast-use instructions. Test availability, conversations, departures, travel, and skipped time.
8. Play the first hour of each opening and one complete learning-to-fieldwork-to-reward loop. Fix repeated gameplay problems before expanding catalogs.

Suggested loop: choose a goal → prepare through study, practice or a relationship → undertake a practical/civic challenge → receive an earned result → see social or institutional consequences → choose the next goal.
