# Narrative Writing and Quest Craft Guide

Apply this authoring guide when developing story starts, narrative arcs, authored quests and significant encounter scenes. It supplements the Worldbuilding Playbook and Runtime Content Boundary Checklist. It is a local workflow guide, not another instruction block to paste into every runtime task.

## Research basis

Writing Excuses treats openings as promises about the experience ahead: choose the information that helps a reader want to continue and defer setup that is not yet useful. For our openings, show the kind of play offered rather than reciting every system. [Writing Excuses: Beginnings](https://writingexcuses.com/writing-excuses-10-14-how-much-of-the-beginning-needs-to-come-first/).

K. M. Weiland recommends introducing a character through pertinent detail and action, with a meaningful immediate scene goal. We adapt that to interactive fiction: establish the situation and available pressure, but let the player choose their own goal and response. [Writing the Opening Scene](https://www.helpingwritersbecomeauthors.com/your-ultimate-first-chapter-checklist-pt-2-writing-the-first-scene/).

The OER fiction-writing collection describes dialogue as an exchange with something at stake; that stake can be understanding or vulnerability rather than a fight. Physical action and brief description can change the rhythm while a person changes tactics. [Character and Dialogue](https://nmoer.pressbooks.pub/creativewriting/chapter/chapter-6-character-and-dialogue/).

These are craft tools, not a mandatory plot formula. A quiet social scene can matter without danger, a cliffhanger or an obligatory secret.

## Before drafting

Read the current place, actual participants, their relationships and knowledge, the player's chosen premise, and any relevant live mechanics. Decide what is different by the end of the scene: an offer is understood, a disagreement becomes concrete, a route opens, trust changes or a practical problem becomes solvable. Do not decide the player's acceptance or emotion in advance.

Write down one immediate question the scene makes interesting. Choose two or three concrete details that serve it. A chalk line, cold cup or worn tool can reveal more than a paragraph of general atmosphere. Keep background facts available for later rather than making the opening an encyclopedia entry.

## Prose and dialogue pass

Start close to an activity, interruption or human concern. Mix action, speech and short description. Use concrete verbs and specific objects; avoid stacked quality adjectives, repeated magical humming, generic awe and a grand metaphor in every paragraph.

Give each speaker a purpose and recognizable cadence. Let a refusal, joke or correction reveal personality. Characters can misunderstand, evade, be selfish or say something foolish. Do not resolve every flaw into hidden kindness. The narrator can make a situation legible without explaining the joke after the dialogue.

Use a few natural action beats, not a blush, smirk, sigh or chin tilt before every line. Vary scene rhythms across a batch. Shared premises do not require the same sequence of beats, identical questions or the same type of minor accident.

For factual records use canonical full names. In dialogue, use clear natural address. Never introduce a replacement identity from a nickname. Preserve academy affiliation and actual years; an older helper is not a first-year simply because the opening needs company.

## Interactive stopping points

A start can establish the walk, timetable and selected welcome as setup. Stop before the player's meaningful reply, seating choice, acceptance, attack or lesson result. A summoned opening can establish awakening and its real bond, but cannot prescribe devotion, obedience, romance or a traveling party.

The player should have enough information to act without needing the narrator to choose for them. Avoid a mandatory menu when ordinary speech works. Routine access and movement should not become artificial obstacles merely to add turns.

## Quest and arc pass

Begin with someone's concrete want and a situation that resists an easy answer. Identify the agreed outcome, interesting obstacle and what credible alternatives change. Failure can change cost, method, timing or relationships without erasing earlier progress.

Do not make every job uncover a conspiracy. A delivery, experiment, reconciliation or paid shift can end at its stated scope. A serious arc earns larger stakes through discoveries and choices. Avoid repeated paperwork and compulsory return reports unless the work actually needs one.

Give each quest one completion owner. Use a typed native completionCondition for a simple outcome. Add explicit stages only when real intermediate evidence, permissions, choices or durable progress matter. Use event triggers for reliable interrupts and mechanical consequences, not as a reflexive substitute for every quest.

Keep the public quest statement about the situation and objective. Put narrator instructions in questDesignBrief; supported state changes belong in effects. An item that preserves memory still needs a natural inventory description.

## Final review

Read the prose aloud. Remove repeated information. Check that the scene's promises fit the actual available gameplay. Verify no player response has been written, no inactive NPC has become present without placement, no new power has appeared for the joke, and no technical instruction is exposed in public text.

Then verify exact addresses, references, grants, conditions and current Studio limits. Local structural checks do not prove the runtime model will recognize the event: test the selected opening and at least one refusal or failure path in Voyage.
