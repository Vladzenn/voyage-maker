# Comparative Reference Atlas

**Checked:** 2026-10-25 (Asia/Shanghai).  
This atlas compares the six read-only examples listed in the [Reference Inventory](Reference%20Inventory.md). It separates what is directly present in those JSON files from current platform behavior documented by Voyage and the community schema reference. The examples are evidence of authoring choices, not templates to copy.

## Findings at a glance

1. **The common building block for classes is a trait category.** None of the exports has a top-level `classes` section. DnD, Konosuba, Larion, and Overlord use a `Class` trait category; Luxcellia uses `Combat Class`; ChikaraOld uses `Invocation Class`. That fits Voyage's trait model: race, class, background, profession, and other build axes all use `traits` grouped by `traitCategories`. Latitude's Closed Beta release also says the platform replaced required race/class fields with flexible traits and starting-item rewards. [Official Closed Beta release](https://voyage.io/releases/closed-beta), [traits reference](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/mechanics/traits), [trait categories reference](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/mechanics/traitCategories).
2. **The examples vary far more in scale than in their top-level systems.** All six share geography, AI instructions, character creation and progression settings, skills, traits, abilities, NPCs, quests, triggers, and other familiar groups. The main design difference is how much content is authored and how much the narrator is asked to generate.
3. **Deterministic mechanics and narrative guidance are mixed in some examples.** Current documentation describes triggers as the reliable way to make state changes happen, while `aiInstructions` guide probabilistic generation. A useful design keeps state transitions in typed mechanics and uses prose guidance for tone, interpretation, and flexible response. [AI instruction reference](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/ai/aiInstructions), [trigger reference](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/mechanics/triggerDefinitions).
4. **Several legacy examples contain reference or scale risks.** DnD has many geography faction labels absent from its faction catalog, a missing class-trait entry, and numerous story-start area mismatches. ChikaraOld has missing NPC-type keys and quest-location references. Larion and Luxcellia contain hundreds of quests with no obvious static activation path. These findings need current Studio validation and, where the system is dynamic, playtesting.
5. **The largest prompts are near current Wiki limits.** Konosuba's `generateNPCIntents` is over the Wiki's current 40,000-character compact-JSON cap in a local compact-JSON count. Several other task prompts sit within a few dozen characters of their caps. Luxcellia's NPC section also appears above the Wiki's 2,000,000-character section cap. These are likely import/publish risks, not confirmed Studio errors.

## Patterns worth reusing as workflow principles

### 1. Make the player-facing choice axis explicit

The examples show different useful axes: class and subclass (DnD), combat role and power origin (ChikaraOld), class plus personality and fatal flaw (Konosuba), race/background/training (Larion), and appearance plus combat/rebirth choices (Luxcellia). These are structures, not content to copy. Current Voyage supports them as ordered `traitCategories` containing trait keys, with `maxSelections`, and `traits` holding their mechanics and narrator guidance. Category order matters for `unlockedBy` and `excludedBy` gating. [Trait categories](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/mechanics/traitCategories), [traits and dependencies](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/mechanics/traits).

### 2. Put class, gear, skill, and ability grants in connected layers

Trait definitions can grant attributes, resources, skill modifiers, starting items, abilities, and narrative effects. Skills determine what gets rolled and which attribute is linked; abilities add an explicitly named action with requirements and effects on the roll. A class-like trait is therefore a bundle of choices and grants, not merely a label. The current reference warns that an ability listed on a trait is granted directly even if the ability's own requirements would normally gate learning it. That dependency should be reviewed before testing. [Traits](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/mechanics/traits), [skills](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/mechanics/skills), [abilities](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/mechanics/abilities).

### 3. Separate catalog data from narrative-only descriptions

The files contain top-level `abilities` for playable actions and also NPC `abilities` arrays. The Wiki explicitly describes NPC ability arrays as narrative prose rather than references to the playable top-level ability catalog. It recommends capability descriptions plus a final fighting-style summary. This explains the long prose arrays in Konosuba, Larion, Luxcellia, and Overlord: treating those strings as broken ability references would be a false positive. [NPC ability format](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/world/npcs#abilities).

### 4. Treat geography as a dependency graph

A location belongs to a region; areas belong to a location; area paths, NPC `currentArea`, story-start locations and areas, quest locations, faction presence, and trigger conditions/effects point back into those maps. DnD illustrates the cost of allowing city names and area names to drift apart: its broad location catalog is useful, but its story-start area pairing is inconsistent in multiple starts. The current Wiki documents that `locationAreas` pairs by array position with `locations`, and that each area is looked up within the paired location. [Locations](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/world/locations), [story starts](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/world/storyStarts), [regions](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/world/regions).

### 5. Surface quests through authored access paths

Kono shows a clean pattern in the static data: each of its 89 authored quests has a `quest-init` trigger. Luxcellia uses many separate quest-progress triggers; Larion has many quest definitions but few direct activation references. Current Wiki guidance says quests start hidden and recommends a story-start `startingQuests` entry or a trigger `quest-init`; its suggested organic pattern is a mechanical arrival gate followed by a story-confirmed discovery gate. [Quests](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/world/quests), [triggers](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/mechanics/triggerDefinitions).

### 6. Keep script volume proportionate to the mechanical need

Larion and Luxcellia show that scripts can support persistent counters, branch logic, and stateful event systems. They also show the resulting cost: reviewers need to trace state keys, trigger order, conditions, and effects across many records. The current Wiki recommends typed trigger conditions/effects first and scripts only for logic they cannot express. This is a maintainability recommendation from the community reference, not a claim that scripts are unsupported. [Trigger scripting and examples](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/mechanics/triggerDefinitions), [scripting patterns](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/appendix/scripting-patterns).

### Character-image prompts: ChikaraOld (reviewed 2026-10-30)

ChikaraOld separates the character profile from the image brief. Its `aiInstructions.generateCharacterBackground.prompt` asks for a gameplay background plus a separate, model-agnostic portrait prompt of up to 5,000 characters. The background carries appearance facts that should matter in play; the portrait-only field carries visual anchors, clothing, accessories, anatomy, expression, pose, composition, art direction, lighting, and background. This keeps visual detail from becoming accidental narrator canon.

Its `imagePromptConfiguration.npcs` then supplies shared art direction and constraints for both NPC and player-character portraits: a consistent cel-shaded anime style, a distinct identity per target, preservation of authored visual facts, controlled variation in face, hair, silhouette, clothing, pose, framing, palette, and light, and exclusions for text, logos, UI, watermarks, unintended anatomy, or extra targets. The reusable pattern is to put stable style and quality rules in one shared instruction, then let each character’s generated appearance specify who they are and what this portrait shows.

This is an observed prompt architecture, not proof that it alone causes the image quality. `ChikaraOld.json` does not identify the rendering model, its settings, rerolls, human selection, or post-processing. The export is an older example, so verify field behavior and image-model selection in current Studio before using it as a schema template. [ChikaraOld source](../sources/ChikaraOld.json), [current Voyage image-model reference](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/other).

### Character-image prompts: Luxcellia (reviewed 2026-10-30)

Luxcellia builds a structured portrait brief from character-creation choices. Its `generateCharacterBackground` task returns separate `Backstory`, `Background`, and `Appearance` fields; gameplay-relevant facts stay in `Background`, while visual selections are routed into the portrait-only `Appearance`. A single optional `Appearance Builder` category groups 14 collapsible axes, including framing, style, body, skin, hair, eyes, outfit, and scene. The category allows 18 total picks; labels such as “recommended: 1” are guidance, not per-subcategory limits. Current Wiki schema documents only the category-wide selection limit, so separate single-select categories are safer when exactly one frame and one style must be chosen. [Luxcellia source](../sources/Luxcellia.json), [trait category schema](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/mechanics/traitCategories).

The prompt instructions define a hierarchy: selected frame controls crop, camera distance, visible body range, scale, pose visibility, and composition; selected style controls rendering and finish; visual traits control the character; scene choice controls the physical environment. The final prompt repeats the frame command, then uses fixed sections for `FRAME AND STYLE`, `CHARACTER`, `SCENE`, `ENVIRONMENT RENDERING`, `VISUAL GOAL`, `SUMMARY TAGS`, and `AVOID`. A marker at the start of `Appearance` tells Luxcellia’s custom NPC image instructions to pass that player prompt through unchanged. This is an observed custom prompt convention, not a native Voyage hard-lock feature.

The screenshots reveal a useful failure case. The supplied prompt calls the selection “Mothlight Offering” but replaces its saved `traitNarrativeEffects` with a generic centered waist-up description. The saved frame’s defining action is a slight three-quarter turn, lowered gaze, raised palm, and one small hovering moth/light. The shown image does not clearly preserve that action. Carry the selected frame’s full defining action into the generated brief; a trait name or repeated crop label alone is not enough. This is evidence of a prompt-content fidelity gap, not proof of an image-model defect. The custom 4,000–4,500-character target and 4,850 stop point are Luxcellia’s own instruction choices, not Voyage limits. Current Voyage notes say portrait cards preserve intended composition, which addresses display cropping rather than guaranteeing that generated art follows every prompt detail. [Official Patch 2.33.7](https://voyage.io/whats-new/patch/patch%3A100000946).

This architecture is more explicit than ChikaraOld’s compact portrait brief plus shared style rules, but its long repeated constraints can become costly and internally inconsistent. Reuse the authority hierarchy, trait-to-section routing, and signature-action fidelity checks; keep the final brief as short as the selected details allow, and verify the image against a visual checklist. Do not reuse the model-specific references in this older export as current model guidance.

### NPC knowledge boundaries: Overlord and Luxcellia (reviewed 2026-10-30)

**Observed in the reference JSONs, not official schema:** [Overlord](../sources/Overlord.json) gives NPC intent an explicit knowledge firewall: first contact does not imply recognition, private scenes and typed player actions are not NPC knowledge, and information needs a plausible channel. Its update instructions also distinguish what a character witnesses from what trusted channels carry. [Luxcellia](../sources/Luxcellia.json) uses a "Who Knows What" summary to retain each secret's audience and the event that exposed it; characters met later start without those private facts.

**Reusable pattern for Glowhaven:** Treat creator-sheet fields and private backstory as narrator-only context. Give each NPC facts only through direct observation, player disclosure, credible reports, or role-appropriate records. Preserve the source and audience in updates and summaries; keep rumor uncertain and appearance-based inference limited. Existing written relationships can establish shared history, but should not grant access to the player's entire profile.

**Failure checks:** At a first meeting, ask about an unshared family history, affinity, motive, or past event and confirm the NPC does not answer as if informed. Tell one NPC a private fact and verify that a distant or absent NPC does not use it until a plausible report reaches them. These are proposed runtime checks, not results already observed.

## Static audit queue

These are the most concrete source-level issues to revisit if an example is used for a structural pattern:

- **DnD:** reconcile 1,747 geography faction labels absent from `factions` (the Wiki says unknown region/location faction keys warn); add or remove the `Blood Hunter` trait-category reference; fix the 47 invalid/unpaired story-start area pairs across 21 starts; inspect the 3 area paths with no matching area; stay aware of 1,449/1,500 playable abilities. The full catalog may be intentionally a setting index, but the current schema treats faction values as keys.
- **ChikaraOld:** fix the three NPC type references (`Musician / Recruitable Ally`, `Technician`, `Licensed Hero`) against the `npcTypes` map; resolve the eleven `questLocation` values (ten use the literal `Unknown`, one uses `Spaceship Crash Site`); review three quests without a static start path. Because quest keys are allowed to differ from quest display names, check every trigger reference against the quest's actual key rather than “fixing” the key/name difference automatically.
- **Larion:** identify intended access paths for the 153 quest definitions without a direct typed/static activation reference. The world has 759 scripts, so inspect script-authored trigger mutations before deciding a quest is inaccessible. Check semantic trigger selection and script budgets in current Studio.
- **Konosuba:** trim or validate `generateNPCIntents` against the 40,000-character compact-JSON cap; several task prompts are at 1–64 characters of margin.
- **Luxcellia:** reduce/verify the NPC section (2,028,832 local compact characters vs 2,000,000 documented); avoid any casual additions to traits, abilities, story starts, or AI task prompts; trace the 59 quests without an obvious static activation path.
- **Overlord:** check whether `A World Without Logout` is intentionally story-generated or needs an explicit authored start path; document why 180 of 229 triggers require scripts.

## What the findings do not establish

A JSON structure can show the intended wiring and many dangling references, but not whether current Voyage accepts the old export shape, whether an AI will follow the prompts reliably, or whether every trigger fires in a real campaign. Those require import into the target Studio release, live validation, and short playtests of the relevant loops. No narrative details, characters, or locations from these examples are adopted as our setting.
