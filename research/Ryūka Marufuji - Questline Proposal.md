# Ryūka Marufuji — You Could Have Asked

**Status:** Historical five-beat proposal, adapted into the Alpha 11 bazaar introduction and two optional quests. Its old opening is not an additional event. The current executable sequence and choices are in [Alpha 11 patch notes](../world-draft/Alpha11%20-%20Ryuka%20Rivalry%20Patch%20Notes.md); the text below preserves the original inspiration.

**Intent:** A school rivalry with real comic chemistry, an invitation that matters more than she admits, and a test of whether her desire to be chosen can survive another person's freedom to choose. Romance is one possible outcome; friendship, a respectful rivalry, a polite refusal, and an unresolved estrangement also work.

## 1. The distinction of her company

**Place:** Dragon Hoard Academy - Student Commons.

After witnessing a useful contribution, a sharp answer, or a modest success by an adult peer, Ryūka Marufuji offers them a place at her table as though she is conferring an honor. Her initial interest can be curiosity, rivalry, or social calculation; this is not instant love. A player from another academy needs a believable reason to be visiting.

She gives a backhanded compliment and suggests a short joint practice session. Accept, ask what she actually wants, return a playful remark, or decline. A civil refusal makes her determined to improve the offer. Publicly humiliating her creates a different, colder encounter rather than awarding more affection.

**Continuity:** Remember whether the player welcomed banter, disliked it, or declined further contact. If they decline the storyline, let her remain a classmate with her own life.

## 2. An entirely ordinary rematch

**Place:** Dragon Hoard Academy - Training Court.

Arrange an approved low-risk exercise under whatever supervision is actually available: strike moving practice targets, intercept harmless training bolts, or spar within agreed stopping conditions. Do not invent a tournament bracket, reward, or faculty appointment. Participation remains optional.

She wants a convincing win and an attentive audience. If she loses, she critiques her own grip in private and demands a rematch aloud. If she wins, she lingers to explain one useful correction, disguising the extra time as impatience with poor standards. If the player spots genuine effort and praises it specifically, she has less prepared to say.

**Choice:** Pursue rivalry, accept practical cooperation, object to a hurtful remark, or leave. A boundary and fair competition can coexist. The result establishes chemistry through an actual exchange rather than a predetermined winner.

## 3. The spare invitation

**Place:** Dragon Hoard Academy - Arrival Gallery, with a later proposed student exhibition at Dragon Hoard Academy - Exhibition Hall.

Ryūka Marufuji produces an invitation to a small, faculty-approved student exhibition and the social gathering afterward. She says there happens to be space beside her demonstration. The neatly prepared invitation and a remembered personal preference suggest she has put more thought into it than that.

The player can accept socially, ask whether this is a personal invitation, invite someone else with her agreement, or decline clearly. If the invitation is mistaken for another taunt, allow a repair through conversation; do not force the misunderstanding to last. She may be privately hurt, but the player does not owe attendance, romance, or consolation.

**Comic beat:** When asked why she did not simply invite them, she can deliver a superbly indignant explanation that fails to answer the question.

## 4. What her name buys

**Place:** Exhibition preparations at Dragon Hoard Academy - Exhibition Hall.

Her household has offered some ordinary materials for the exhibition. Ryūka Marufuji has treated that contribution as a reason to give her demonstration the most favorable placement and leave another student's practical contribution out of the presentation. The contribution is real and useful; the unfair allocation is also real. No secret curse or hidden villain is needed.

The other student objects. Review the preparations and speak to the people involved. The player can support a correction, discuss a fair arrangement, decline to intervene, or flatter Ryūka Marufuji and benefit from the favoritism. Madoka Narukami can address an actual classroom or exhibition dispute within her teaching role; she does not magically detect wrongdoing or pronounce a citywide sentence.

Ryūka Marufuji first argues that this is how support is obtained. Someone whose opinion she values can tell her precisely what she did and why it matters. Affection does not settle the dispute. She can restore credit and share the placement, cling to her advantage, or make a partial correction that leaves the relationship strained.

## 5. Say what you meant

**Place:** After the exhibition, along Dragon Hoard Academy - Arrival Gallery.

Give the people involved room to react to what actually happened. If she repaired the unfairness, she can admit that the evening was supposed to make her look impressive to someone in particular. If she refused, the conversation must acknowledge that refusal rather than skipping to a tender reward scene. The player can also decline a private conversation.

With sufficient trust, she tries one direct sentence: she wanted their company. She can ask for another walk, another practice session, or a date when mutual adult romantic interest has already become clear. A nonromantic answer can be kind and final. She is allowed to be disappointed and retain her dignity.

The comic payoff is a familiar boast returning after an honest moment. She can be pleased, proud, and insufferable without retracting everything sincere she just said.

## Outcomes and follow-through

- **Chosen friend:** Shared practice, personal invitations, reliable favors, and teasing that respects established limits. She gradually stops presenting every kindness as a transaction.
- **Respected rival:** Competition remains central. She credits the other's strengths and can accept a loss without reaching immediately for her family's influence.
- **Mutual romance:** Only after reciprocal adult interest and an accepted invitation. Private warmth and public embarrassment coexist with continued rivalry. Jealousy becomes something to discuss rather than a reason to control another person's friendships.
- **Polite distance:** A refusal remains a refusal. She can be awkward or briefly prickly, then develop other interests rather than becoming a mandatory recurring pursuer.
- **Unrepaired harm:** The affected student need not forgive her, and the player can withdraw trust. Repair later requires correcting the particular action, not delivering a grand confession.
- **Complicity:** If the player supports favoritism, their closeness can grow around a selfish shared choice. That is a valid darker branch with social consequences, not a secret good ending disguised as romance.

Possible rewards are access to mutually agreed practice, useful social introductions she can actually arrange, companionship, and opportunities to study a technique through normal learning. No spell, romance, unlimited patronage, or privileged access is automatically awarded.

For narrative continuity, retain what was invited, what answer was given, who witnessed the unfairness, what she repaired, and any relationship boundary. These are plain authoring notes, not invented JSON field names. Do not restart the same invitation, jealousy lesson, or apology after it has already been resolved.
