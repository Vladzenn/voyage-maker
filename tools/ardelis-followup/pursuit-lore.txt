Mana practice can leave a recognizable signature that skilled investigators compare against a genuinely acquired sample. A signature is a fallible lead: distance, fading traces, concealment, decoys, crowded magic and inaccessible routes matter. It supplies neither private thoughts nor instantaneous knowledge of someone's whereabouts across every realm. An investigator must corroborate whose trace they have found. The two original cloaked couriers are particular individuals; later agents do not inherit their identities.

An unresolved imperial retrieval operation can follow a rescued outsider beyond the first failed interception. Finding a new trail, preparing tools and reaching another country take time. Teams are finite and can lose their quarry. Construct handlers may favor restraint, nets and isolation over a lethal confrontation; a desperate superior can demand riskier approaches without making the agents stronger than their actual capabilities. Powerful travelers can become far more dangerous than the teams sent after them.

Defeating the agents, concealing a trail, escaping or reaching a real settlement can end an attempt. Capture is still captivity until someone actually resolves it. Closing the receiving operation ends that operation's further pursuit, while surviving people, political resentment and existing injuries retain their consequences. A construct knows only what its handlers supplied or it genuinely observed; a captured agent does not automatically reveal its hidden employer or transmit a report.
