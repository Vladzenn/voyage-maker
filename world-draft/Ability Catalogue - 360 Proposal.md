# Ability Catalogue — Proposed 360-Ability Programme

Status: a review plan, not an implemented spell library. The world currently has ten authored abilities. The target of 360 includes those ten, leaving approximately 350 new entries; it is a flexible target, not a quota to fill with duplicates. Time receives no public batch. Spellcraft is an underlying invention skill, not an ability players must purchase before they can invent anything.

## What makes twelve spells worth having

For a typical discipline, aim for three introductory, three trained, two advanced, two expert and two master techniques. Suggested training bands are 1–19, 20–39, 40–59, 60–79 and 80–100. These are planning bands, not a new level system or automatic acquisition. Space can begin only after Gravity preparation; Time has no public route.

The twelve must offer different reasons to use them. A useful mix might contain direct pressure, an alternative attack geometry, protection, movement, control, exploration, a practical task and a demanding signature working. Do not force Healing to have a fireball equivalent or Sound to duplicate Lightning. Utility can be beautiful and useful without pretending it wins a battle. A modest technique can remain valuable at mastery because of precision, cost, setup or a particular interaction.

The library is not an exclusive action menu. A trained person can attempt ordinary skill applications without purchasing a card for every parry, jump, elemental pulse or useful cantrip. Named abilities add a distinctive repeatable method or benefit. Basic attacks, routine guard and ordinary movement stay available without Stamina; demanding martial maneuvers spend it. A fighter should have real decisions about reach, cover, grapples, footing, interruption, equipment and allies even when no spell is cast.

## Naming philosophy

Give the cast spells they would enjoy announcing. Use a specific image, motion or consequence, then make the effect understandable in plain language. Everyday spells can be short; an advanced signature can have a theatrical name. Rotate structures rather than stamping every entry with the same adjective or rank word.

- **Cinder Hook:** a flame release following one practiced curved line, useful around a predictable angle.
- **Sevenfold Sunlance:** a deliberate array of converging light beams; dramatic staging with a real charge and firing lane.
- **Glasswinter Procession:** a moving sequence of ice structures controlling a chosen route rather than freezing the entire battlefield.
- **The Unquiet Witness:** an attempted conversation with a particular conscious spirit, whose actual knowledge, agency and condition matter.
- **Requiem Marionette:** a different Soul design: a limited animated corpse without automatically recalling the deceased person’s soul.
- **Door Between Raindrops:** a precise short spatial displacement, with a clear endpoint and a skill-appropriate carried burden.
- **Towerfall Shoulder:** a forceful physical entry with trained leverage; the name does not secretly grant Earth magic.
- **Kingfisher Lunge:** a precise change of line and reach; the bird is imagery, not a summoned animal.

These are naming/effect pitches, not authored entries or promises of automatic success. Avoid numbered upgrades, universal “Ultimate/Sovereign” prefixes, arbitrary armor bypass and repeated “X but larger” designs. A memorable name does not have to be unique in every individual word; a sensible naming family is allowed without creating an evolution tree.

## Spell invention and ordinary learning

An ordinary mage can say, “I want this familiar flame to curve toward a point I can still see.” The design attempt uses Spellcraft, informed by their actual Fire knowledge. If a simple experiment succeeds and establishes a reproducible method, it can become a personal spell. A difficult design requires proportionate research and tests. The eventual cast uses Fire Magic. It does not need a second Spellcraft check every time, and inventing it does not grant a new affinity.

Learning someone else’s prepared spell is a different path. Authored catalogue entries use native prerequisites and ability points or explicit trait grants. The personal generator creates original character abilities rather than distributing existing catalogue names. A cosmetic nickname can remain flavor without another card. Our four-turn generated-learning cadence does not guarantee a discovery every four turns.

## Card design and engine fit

The authoring worksheet records effect, delivery, tactical purpose, target, range, duration, cost, counterplay, prerequisites and apparent style. Those are design notes. Export only supported ability fields: name, description, requirements, bonus and cooldown. Scope and costs belong in the description/resource guidance; do not invent range, cost, status, damage, multiattack or concentration fields.

Use positive effects first, followed by enough precision to run the move. A concise limitation is useful; a paragraph mostly saying what a spell cannot do is not an exciting spell. Prerequisites gate eligibility, not guaranteed success. Physical hybrids name both the delivery skill and relevant discipline, while resolving one primary action rather than summing two entire skill totals.

Before importing a batch: check exact names and references; avoid duplicates; compare real benefits and costs across physical and magical options; test one new or under-equipped character, one trained specialist and one hybrid. Spatial travel, active restoration, summons and temporary matter deserve focused live tests before expanding their families.
