# Drakenspire — Map and Environment Art Brief

## The composition to preserve

Drakenspire is a snowy alpine fortress city on two nearby mountain shelves. **The higher northeastern shelf holds Crescent Crown Fortress, almost wrapped by an enormous ancestral dragon skeleton in a crescent. The bustling city occupies the broader, slightly lower shelf below and southwest of it.** Fortress and bones form one unmistakable hero landmark. Keep enough inhabited density to read as a city, but make individual buildings and government interiors practical rather than uniformly grand.

The nation and capital are both called Drakenspire. The Voyage realm key remains **Drakenspire Mountains**, with **Drakenspire Highlands** as its existing region. This map covers the capital and surrounding expedition country, not every part of the nation or an entire continent.

The map's density comes from many useful modest places, houses and work yards. Three clanholds are substantial households, not three additional palaces. A schoolhouse is one medium building; shops can share streets and blocks.

## Geographic arrangement

| Map zone | Intended geography | Important visible anchors |
|---|---|---|
| High northeast | A distinctly higher shelf, reached by a rising guarded switchback | Crescent Crown Fortress and the ancestral skeleton; inner courts, hall roofs, sanctuary and ramparts |
| Center and southwest below the fortress | The main inhabited shelf, visibly lower but close enough to belong to the same city | Dense stone homes, broad procession route, central market, warm windows, a few colorful awnings |
| Working city edge | A practical freight approach with access to roads and the arrival quarter | Foundry chimneys, covered loading yards, workshops and the enclosed portal hall |
| Lower sheltered ledges | Suitable ground beneath the busiest streets | One onsen with controlled steam, waterworks, livestock pens, stores and small pasture folds |
| Overland approach | A descending road that can be defended and cleared | Lower Hold Gate, caravan lodge, watchpost, Goatstep Hamlet and winding trail markers |
| Outer mountain margins | A neighboring peak, exposed passes, ravines and limited habitable folds | Frostback Mountain, Rimecrest Trail, Glassmere Basin, sparse Pinefold Hollow |
| Working and abandoned slopes | Rock faces accessible from a road or old haul route | Redvein Mine, amber cavern entrance, broken chain lift and abandoned Hearthfall roofs |
| Remote outer country | Visibly more isolated and demanding terrain | Wyvern crags, giant-scale traces, snowy basins and old ruins without making each a giant landmark |

North is the top of the intended composition and east the right. The JSON's regular grid is only a temporary editor layout. It does not encode these geographic placements or realistic travel distances; move the markers after choosing the map.

## The sacred crescent

Make the skeleton enormous enough that the fortress feels built beside and within its protection, while the bones remain identifiable as one dragon's remains. A sweeping backbone and rising ribs can describe the crescent around the upper rock; the skull should be readable as part of the same body. The public walkways sit alongside the bones, with respectful boundaries and small maintenance structures.

The bones are ancient physical remains with historical and religious significance. They do not emit a city-heating aura, grow crystal buildings, power the portal or form a floating magical barrier. Avoid duplicating skulls or implying several skeletons. Keep the fortress walls and the skeleton visually distinct rather than turning every wall into bone.

## Palette and atmosphere

Suggested art treatment: blue-white snow and cool distant mountains; charcoal and pale-gray stone; dark blue-gray slate; warm amber windows; selective copper, russet, wool-red and muted teal accents. This is a palette proposal, not a rigid national uniform or approved heraldry.

Use sunlight and warm inhabited details to keep the city colorful and inviting despite the cold. Forge smoke, a few awnings, food stalls, roof texture and festival-ready street widths give it life. Snow lies naturally on roofs and ledges, with cleared roads and practical drains. Do not cover every street in equally pristine untouched snow or make the entire country look abandoned.

The government quarter is restrained. Coppercrest Exchange may stand out through richer trim and taller windows, but should not rival the fortress in silhouette. The Cinder Pit is a small enclosed fighting venue, not a second coliseum. The onsen is a single sheltered complex, not a field of giant steaming pools. Pine growth is sparse and concentrated in suitable folds.

## Dragon and inhabitants

The living guardian is gray, enormous, intelligent and capable of lightning breath and spellcasting, with **four legs and two separate wings**. If a composition includes him, a distant sky silhouette is sufficient. He is not perched on the fortress for public audiences, chained to a gate or shown guarding an identified cave. The author has not established his lair. Ordinary maps should not mark one.

Dragonkin background people remain small at regional map scale. Where anatomy is readable, use human faces, dragonlike horns, lateral facial scales, slit pupils, scaly backs of hands and arms, human-looking palms and thick tails. People are roughly human-height and vary in color and scale coverage. Local clothes use practical wool, fur and layers; not everyone is armed or armored. Other peoples can appear naturally in the city.

Wyverns are smaller, two-legged winged creatures; their nesting country belongs beyond the inhabited shelves. Do not substitute a wyvern silhouette for the six-limbed guardian. No prominent named NPC portrait belongs in the regional map.

## Detail and label discipline

The 60 location markers represent playable destinations. Several share a larger complex or district: the Jarl's Hall and assembly belong to the upper precinct; market shops occupy normal blocks; service rooms are areas inside their parent location. A regional illustration does not need 60 isolated monumental buildings or visible underground floor plans.

Use exterior cues for cave entrances, mine works and ruins. Do not expose every interior or disclose obscure sites with glowing arrows. The four initially unknown sites are First Shelter Ruins, The White Silence, Coldglass Galleries and The Fallen Pass Shrine. Their terrain can exist in the landscape without readable labels or a conspicuous reward marker.

For the base artwork: no text, lettering, map labels, UI, logos, signatures or watermarks. Keep the fortress-crescent landmark comfortably inside the image bounds. Leave enough terrain around the inhabited city for the dangerous outer routes to make visual sense.
