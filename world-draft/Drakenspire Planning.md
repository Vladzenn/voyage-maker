# Drakenspire — National Lore and Map Planning

Status: historical questionnaire, answered by the author on 2026-10-28. The completed Alpha 8 expansion is documented in Drakenspire - National Lore.md, Drakenspire - Location Catalogue.md and Drakenspire - Map Art Brief.md. The questions and suggestions below preserve the earlier planning record; they do not override the approved answers or current world. In particular, the chosen skeleton is historical/religious, and the dragon's lair is unknown.

## Confirmed direction

- Drakenspire is another nation with its own culture.
- Expand it to nearly Glowhaven's scale. The current baseline is Glowhaven 67 locations / 273 areas, Drakenspire 5 / 16. A working proposal is about 60 locations / 240–280 areas, adjustable after the answers.
- Place the expanded location coordinates on a temporary grid for manual arrangement later. Logical route descriptions should describe the intended country; the grid is an editor layout, not finalized geography or travel distance.
- Give the nation one major landmark that defines its skyline and visual identity.
- A dragon lives here. Its role, identity, nature and home require the author's decisions.
- Author the lore and location/area descriptions before generating the map. The resulting landmark/building inventory will guide a map in the same style as Glowhaven's.
- New regional NPC rosters come later. Current work can describe services and institutional functions without inventing their named personnel.
- Dragonkin anatomy follows Dragonkin Appearance Guide.md and the matching world lore; no anatomy block is added to category-wide image prompts.

The current saved realm has a snowy mountain capital, a dragonkin majority, basalt/stone/cobbled construction, warriors and metalworking, plus five existing places: Drakenspire, Embersteel Foundries, Stonefang Training Grounds, Whitepass Caravan Lodge and Rimecrest Trail. These are the starting material; the author may develop or revise them.

## Questions and suggestions

1. **How much country should this map cover?** Is this the capital and its immediate mountains, or a larger national map with several towns and distinct regions? I suggest a dense capital, a few outlying settlements and a substantial ring of wilderness, so sixty locations include daily life as well as travel.

2. **What are the nation and capital called?** Drakenspire can name both, or it can be the capital of a differently named country. Keeping one shared name is simple; a separate national name can emphasize an older kingdom, alliance of clans or realm founded around the dragon.

3. **What should arriving here feel like visually and emotionally?** Think proud alpine fortress-city, extravagant dragon court, cozy snowbound trade capital, or a mixture you describe. Dark stone, colored roof tiles, copper roofs, covered streets and warm windows could make it imposing from afar and lively at street level.

4. **What is the huge landmark?** Possibilities include the Worldforge, a colossal forge built into the mountain; Skyfang, an impossible black spire through the clouds; or an immense ancient dragon skeleton incorporated into the city. Another direction is a crystalline glacier-crown with inhabited terraces beneath it—pick an image you want the whole map to revolve around.

5. **What does that landmark do for people?** It might provide heat, protect the city, enable extraordinary craft, or simply hold enormous historic and religious significance. I like a useful landmark with ordinary consequences: its warmth shapes gardens, bathhouses and housing, while its prestige shapes who wants to live near it.

6. **What is the resident dragon's place in society?** Ruler, protector, founder, sacred neighbor, independent landlord, or an inconvenient ancient resident? A respected guardian with demands could create different politics from a dragon monarch; either can have a surprisingly ordinary relationship with the people who clean, feed or negotiate with it.

7. **What kind of dragon, and where does it live?** What scale/color, element, age, size and general temperament do you picture, and can it take a humanoid form? Its lair could crown the main landmark, occupy a neighboring peak, or sit beneath the city, with its physical size affecting approaches, gates and public viewing places.

8. **Who actually governs the country?** A monarch, dragon sovereign, council of clans, military leadership, merchant assembly or another arrangement? If the dragon is an ancient protector rather than an administrator, a mortal government handling courts, streets and trade gives both sides meaningful roles.

9. **Who has status, wealth and influence?** Is prestige inherited, earned through craft or combat, bought, or negotiated through clan and guild ties? Noble houses with differing interests, wealthy workshops and working households can create more texture than a single ladder where the strongest person owns everything.

10. **What else should be true of dragonkin bodies and heritage?** Do they normally have wings or breath attacks, how long do they live, and does color mean anything beyond appearance? My starting suggestion is visual variety without automatically assigning an affinity or personality from scale color; unusual powers can belong to specific lineages or individuals if you want them.

11. **What naming and language conventions do you want?** Should this country use Japanese-inspired names, another recognizable linguistic influence, or a distinctive fantasy register? We can keep first-name–last-name presentation while letting names sound local; Draconic also needs a decision about how to represent it in readable dialogue.

12. **How was Drakenspire founded, and what history still matters?** A bargain with the dragon, refugee clans sheltering in a mountain, a forge settlement that became a kingdom, or a conquered citadel could all work. One founding event and one later disagreement would give us monuments, ruins and different interpretations without requiring an enormous timeline.

13. **What do people believe in?** Is the dragon worshipped, respected as a powerful neighbor, or mostly separate from religion? Ancestor halls, hearth rites, mountain spirits and a few competing traditions could create sacred places and ordinary family customs without making every resident equally devout.

14. **How do people learn magic, craft and fighting?** Formal schools, guild apprenticeships, clan instruction, military colleges or a mixture? I suggest an education system with its own shape—such as a major craft institution and independent fighting schools—so the nation feels different from Glowhaven's four-academy structure.

15. **What does the country make, trade and spend?** Metalwork is already a foothold; should its specialty be extraordinary weapons, durable enchanted equipment, precision tools, mining, gems or something else? Decide whether local money is compatible with Glowhaven's marks or needs exchange, and what the country must import to support mountain life.

16. **How do warmth, food and water work in the snow?** Geothermal springs, heated terraces, sheltered valleys, imports and hardy livestock can combine in different ways. This choice gives us useful places—greenhouses, bathhouses, reservoirs, storehouses and freight stations—rather than snow serving only as decoration.

17. **What are law enforcement, the military and public dueling like?** Who keeps streets safe, guards passes, fights monsters and handles crimes, and how formal are challenges? Regulated bouts and a serious mountain-rescue service could express martial pride while leaving everyday residents free to live ordinary lives.

18. **What are the everyday manners and social embarrassments?** How do people greet, host, court, argue or show respect, and what would a visitor get wrong? Tail-friendly furniture, elaborate coat etiquette, competitive hospitality or fierce arguments about proper forge-side meals could provide comedy grounded in the place.

19. **What do people eat, wear and do for fun?** Give me foods, clothing, music, crafts, festivals, sports or nightlife you want to see. Spiced stews, bright winter fabrics, ice-racing, public baths and a forge festival are possibilities; choose the pleasures that make you want to spend a peaceful evening here.

20. **How does Drakenspire regard Glowhaven and foreign visitors?** Friendly partner, rival, useful trading relationship, political tension or a mix? I like mutual dependence with opinions attached: people can admire Glowhaven's enchanted work and still make jokes about its students, while the portal remains a real agreement between independent authorities.

21. **What lies beyond the capital?** Which places should shape the wider map: mines, pine forests, frozen lakes, high passes, hot-spring valleys, forts, farms, monasteries or villages? A few contrasting landscapes and settlements will make journeys feel different and give the future map a recognizable silhouette.

22. **What dangers and old sites belong here?** Weather, avalanches, territorial beasts, abandoned mines, ruins, bandits, rival powers or supernatural threats? I suggest distinct dangerous pockets with clear signs and safe inhabited centers; then we can choose ruins or dungeons with their own histories instead of making every road an ambush.

23. **What should players especially enjoy doing here?** Commissioning equipment, fighting, studying, climbing, finding relics, hunting monsters, pursuing local work or getting drawn into society? Pick several priorities and their rough balance, and I can turn them into places with practical interactions while preserving quieter shops, homes and scenic stops.

24. **How public is the map, and which places should feel exceptional?** Should travelers know most districts and landmarks on arrival, with only a handful of secret sites hidden? I suggest public markets, services and famous monuments be readable, while a concealed ruin or private dragon route has a real discovery process; also tell me any must-have place or aesthetic you have not found a home for above.

Answer by number in whatever amount of detail suits you. “Pick for me” is a valid answer. Suggestions above stay proposals until accepted; unresolved points can be handled together before authoring the large location batch.

## Planned output after answers

Develop the approved answers into scoped world-lore entries, a national/realm overview, an institution and service inventory, a location/area catalogue, sensible paths and level bands, and a map-art brief identifying the major landmark, districts, approaches and surrounding terrain. Place authored locations on a temporary grid while preserving existing images and coherent portal endpoints. Do not create the regional NPC cast or generate the map as part of this initial questionnaire.
