This document develops the author's decisions and our brainstorming into proposed lorebook content for a future minor patch after Champions of the Crucible. The expansion is substantial in narrative scope and light in gameplay. It adds context for the wider world, not new gameplay systems, mapped destinations, quests, NPC cards, abilities, ancestry benefits, artwork or working portals.

## How to use this document

The **Proposed in-world lorebook entries** contain general setting prose. The **Private author account** may also be included in the eventual lorebook as explicitly labeled author/narrator-only background. Its presence in the lorebook does not make it information available within the setting. **No in-game character or source knows or reveals the primordial beginnings.** This includes NPCs, celestials, gods, books, research, histories, records and every other in-game source. The narrator may use this background for consistency but must never disclose it during play.

The national concepts were accepted in discussion. Their detailed development below is draft connective prose, not a claim that every descriptive detail was separately approved. Ardelis, Valcéranne and Dunbrae carry forward the names from the accepted sketches; Yunhai already appears in existing lore. Borders, capitals, rulers, dates and continent names remain unassigned.

This is an expansion and reconciliation of the current lorebook, not a replacement for unrelated entries. Existing academies, Drakenspire, Asterveil, the Crucible, the Conservatory and magic remain established. The Evertree's founding mystery and Asterveil ruins remain unexplained. The Spider Realm remains exact.

## Confirmed decisions

- The world is **Earth**, approximately real-Earth size, with different continental shapes. Apart from magic, ordinary cosmic and physical principles apply: planets orbit the Sun; stars, galaxies and an immense universe exist.
- Peoples and countries occupy widely separated lands, including different continents. Ships, roads, navies, ports and long journeys remain important.
- Glowhaven is a famous magical center, commonly considered the largest. Other countries have smaller competing institutions.
- **The four founders made Glowhaven.** Ardelis's royalty did not found it. Glowhaven has cut off its former relationship of subordination and no longer answers to Ardelis or its crown. The date and circumstances remain undecided.
- Permanent portals are extraordinarily expensive and require enormous magic. The Evertree sustains several distant connections: an exceptional breakthrough. Glowhaven is the hub. Its mages established the Drakenspire endpoint using the Evertree; that endpoint does not demonstrate Drakenspire's own portal-making technology.
- Magic follows the same underlying principles worldwide. Cultural casting and teaching traditions vary. Glowhaven favors educational, scientific and formulaic methods.
- Elvish is represented as French. Some cities and mannerisms have French inspiration, while the broader elven direction is Tolkien-like.
- Elves live about **1,000 years**, physically mature at roughly the human rate, and retain a youthful adult appearance. Their mana changes over time and is the principal indication of their age.
- The author said elves are “not considered social adults at 100.” The exact cultural adulthood threshold needs clarification; the draft does not supply one.
- Dwarves have the recognizable stereotypical Scottish accent. Mountain communities coexist with substantial maritime societies and traditions of skilled, demanding work.
- The Valcéranne elven kingdom and Dunbrae dwarven maritime commonwealth concepts were accepted.
- **Yunhai is populated by various types of beastfolk**, with regional and individual diversity.
- Ardelis introduces worship of a celestial deity. The separate fifth celestial-worshipping nation is deferred.
- Gods are real powerful beings rather than primordial creators. Celestials can look angelic or eldritch and be extremely ancient. Some claim to have created the world. Other cultures honor spirits, ancestors or mythical beasts.
- There are many histories of rising and falling civilizations, caused by invasions, monsters and other plausible events. No single universal ancient civilization is established.
- Different countries support varied moods: ordinary warmth, wonder, adventure, politics and darker mysteries.
- Entirely new original peoples remain possible. Neither mineral-bodied nor adult-metamorphosis people were approved.
- A literal Hell is deferred. Its nature, inhabitants, access and relation to death are undefined.
- The later Oni culture/society expansion remains separate. Existing homeland context can be polished here without adding its map or opening the torii route.
- The wider world need not revolve around existing named characters. Residents, correspondence, goods and ordinary conversation can carry its context.

## Private author account — never known or revealed in-game

After the Big Bang, primordial eldritch beings came into existence. Their numbers are nigh infinite. They are incomprehensible to mortal minds and do not think in human terms.

They began making galaxies, stars and planets, initially empty. Their works included gases, elements, matter and the fundamental physics and laws by which creation behaves. There are trillions of planets and stars. Earth is one of the relatively few populated worlds.

Primordial speech is a major part of primordial magic. A single breath can carry thousands of years of information and influence reality. It is not an ordinary ancient language that scholars can translate from a surviving dictionary.

A group of primordials made Earth as an experiment and diversion. One sacrificed himself out of **curiosity** to bring life into the world. This is the actual motive; humanlike altruism, martyrdom, cosmic duty and a struggle against evil have not been established.

His act produced Earth's abundance of creatures and suffused it with lingering primordial power. The fraction accessible to those creatures is now called mana. This origin does not replace the existing distinctions between usable mana, the soul's sustaining reserve, ordinary animals, mana beasts and intentional casting.

**No being in this world knows these beginnings.** This includes mortals, celestials, worshipped gods, oracles and other ancient inhabitants. No exception has been approved for a secret cult, forbidden library, divine revelation, primordial memory, archaeological inscription or exceptional Divination spell.

This account may be stored in the lorebook as private author/narrator background, explicitly identified as unknown and unavailable in-world. It must never become NPC knowledge, a book passage, research finding, historical account, inscription, revelation, direct narrator disclosure during play or any other in-game source of the truth. It is not a discoverable secret. In-world creation stories remain beliefs and claims. Celestials claiming creation do not acquire the truth. Their exact origins and how much they believe their claims remain open.

This account does not explain the Evertree's founding or Asterveil's ruins. Those mysteries remain separate.

## Proposed in-world lorebook entries

The following headings are candidate entry names. The text beneath them is setting prose; production instructions and unresolved choices belong elsewhere.

### Earth and the Wider World

Earth is a vast world of continents, islands and oceans beneath a sky of planets, stars and distant galaxies. It revolves around the Sun. Its lands span many climates, from cold mountain countries and temperate agricultural plains to warm coasts, dry interiors and humid forests. Magic forms part of the world alongside weather, geology, ecosystems and the movement of the heavens.

The regions familiar to Glowhaven occupy only a small part of Earth. Farther lands have their own great cities, remote settlements, centers of learning, political struggles and remembered histories. Their inhabitants make journeys and tell stories in which Glowhaven may be an important destination, a distant name or scarcely a concern.

### Continents, Nations and Peoples

The peoples of Earth are distributed across broad homelands, centuries of migration and communities established far from their origins. Some countries have a clear majority of one people. Ports, border regions and centers of study often attract many ancestries.

An elven kingdom can contain human households, dwarven traders and foreign students. Dwarven sailors can have deep roots thousands of miles from their grandparents' homeland. Beastfolk include many bodies and family traditions within one country. Language, upbringing, religion and allegiance shape community identity alongside ancestry.

Countries also contain regional differences. A capital's fashions, farming district's customs and coastal town's loyalties need not coincide. Family origin gives context without settling a person's character.

### Maps, Distance and Foreign Knowledge

Maps preserve what people have surveyed, measured or learned through travel and reports. Their accuracy varies with purpose, age and reliable information. A harbor merchant may know foreign coastlines better than the interior of their own continent. A precise local survey can end where older travelers' accounts begin.

Established roads, inhabited countries and well-charted waters extend far beyond Glowhaven's familiar routes. A place unfamiliar to visitors may be intimately known to its residents. Ships, couriers, correspondence, visitors and established magical connections carry news at different speeds and with different degrees of accuracy.

### Oceans, Ships and Naval Power

Oceans separate major parts of the inhabited world. Merchant shipping carries food, materials, manufactured goods, passengers and correspondence along routes sustained by experienced crews and working ports. Fishing settlements, shipyards, markets and inland roads connect those voyages to people who never go to sea.

Navies protect or contest sea lanes, defend harbors, escort traffic and carry a country's force abroad. Weather, reefs, hostile fleets and dangerous creatures shape a voyage. Navigation, provisions, shipbuilding and repair remain substantial work even where magic assists. A famous port can be known through accounts long before someone has the means to visit it.

### Permanent Portals and Glowhaven's Hub

Establishing and sustaining a permanent portal requires immense magical power, costly work and specialist knowledge. Such connections are exceptional undertakings. Most journeys and freight still depend on ships, roads and ordinary infrastructure.

The Evertree makes Glowhaven extraordinary. Its power sustains several distant connections through the city's portal hub, a revolutionary achievement in practical magic. Glowhaven's mages established the Drakenspire connection using that power. Its receiving endpoint does not mean Drakenspire independently possesses equivalent portal-making expertise.

The hub also serves the established Asterveil route. Authorized passages connect the actual arrival rooms with Glowhaven's Portal Room, bringing distant communities into frequent contact while much of the world remains beyond their reach.

### Glowhaven Among the Centers of Magic

Glowhaven is widely known for magical education, scholarship and craftsmanship, commonly regarded as the world's largest center. The Evertree and four academies draw students, researchers, skilled workers and visitors from distant countries. Graduates, enchanted goods, correspondence and collaboration extend its reputation.

Other lands have respected institutions and teaching traditions. Scholars can admire Glowhaven's facilities, seek particular specialists, disagree with its methods or prefer education closer to home. Foreign institutions compete for discoveries, patrons and students. Their standing reflects actual work and local history as well as what Glowhaven knows of them.

### Magical Principles and Cultural Traditions

Magic follows shared principles throughout the world. A familiar operation can be taught through different terminology, bodily habits, instruments and traditions. Instruction develops in schools, households, workshops, religious communities and individual apprenticeships.

Glowhaven favors explicit theory, practiced blueprints, repeatable exercises and precise explanations. Elsewhere, comparable knowledge may be taught through recitation, ritual practice or other approaches. A successful unfamiliar technique can invite study without implying different physical laws.

Affinity, learned competence, usable capacity and skilled control remain distinct. A culture's reputation does not grant every member the same affinity or ability.

### Histories of Kingdoms and Cities

Earth preserves many histories of settlement, union, division and collapse. Kingdoms have grown from alliances, expanded through conquest and broken apart in succession disputes. Cities have been abandoned after changing trade routes, failed harvests, destructive wars or attacks by dangerous creatures. Others survived and changed around such events.

The material past belongs to many peoples and periods. A ruined fort, an old road and a buried town can have entirely different origins. Records, testimony, objects and sites support historical knowledge alongside disputed interpretations. These histories need not belong to one universal ancient empire.

### Glowhaven — Founders and Independence

The four great Archmages whose followers established Phoenix Wing Academy, Hydra Head Academy, Gryffin Tail Academy and Dragon Hoard Academy founded Glowhaven. Their work belongs to the city's own beginnings. Four Sages Bazaar's monument remains a public reminder.

Glowhaven no longer answers to Ardelis or its royalty. Its former political connection has been cut, and it conducts the affairs of an independent polity. The Great Sage governs through Glowhaven's own institutions. Ardelis's crown has no standing authority over the city or its academies.

The legend that the founders planted the Evertree together remains part of civic memory. Its exact circumstances and the tree's deeper nature remain uncertain.

### Glowhaven — Foreign Relations

Glowhaven's government handles its relations with foreign countries. The Great Sage's court oversees authorized diplomacy, official visitors and agreements for travel and exchange. Academies, merchants, craftspeople and residents maintain international connections within actual arrangements.

Ardelis remains part of the city's historical background, but former ties do not make its royalty Glowhaven's rulers. Trade, study, family and friendship can continue across political boundaries.

Drakenspire's alliance and Asterveil's practical treaty retain their established character. The portal hub connects independent communities with their own governments and interests.

### Ardelis — Crown, Country and Provinces

Ardelis is a large, predominantly human kingdom of agricultural provinces, old administrative cities, coastal ports and noble estates. Farmers, merchants, craftspeople, scholars, soldiers and households of other ancestries inhabit it. Provincial life varies with the land, history and access to wealth.

The crown, noble houses, municipal offices and religious institutions hold different kinds of influence. Roads, taxation, military service, commerce and land bring their interests together. A prosperous port may have priorities very different from those of an inland estate or royal capital.

Glowhaven is independent. Its reputation and former connection interest the kingdom, but Ardelis's royalty neither founded nor governs the city.

### Ardelis — Celestial Worship

An ancient celestial deity holds a prominent place in Ardelis's religion. Temples, ceremonies and household observances connect worship with civic and private life. Royal ceremony draws on this tradition, while religious institutions have histories of patronage, teaching, assistance and political influence.

The deity is a real supernatural being. Accounts of its character, deeds and place in creation pass through sacred histories and interpretation. Faith varies between households and regions, as does trust in particular temples.

People can share a religion while disagreeing about rulers, priests or teachings. Some find devotion comforting, others observe family custom, and others hold different beliefs.

### Ardelis — Glowhaven and the Former Connection

Ardelis and Glowhaven have a former political relationship that no longer grants the kingdom authority over the city. Glowhaven's founders and institutions remain central to its own identity.

People on either side view that history differently. A noble family may retain private connections; a trader may care chiefly about dependable passage; a scholar may value colleagues across the border. Present dealings rest on actual agreements and circumstances. Older associations are history rather than continuing royal jurisdiction.

### Elves — Lifespan, Maturity and Appearance

Elves live for roughly a thousand years. They physically mature at approximately the human rate, then retain an adult appearance through lives far longer than human ones. Their faces and bodies do not ordinarily show the familiar progression of human visible aging.

Elven communities distinguish physical maturity from much later cultural recognition of full adult standing. A young, physically mature elf may work, travel or study while older relatives regard them as inexperienced. Experience and local expectations shape those relationships.

Youthful appearance is a poor guide to an elf's years. Knowledge still reflects what a person has actually learned, remembered and done.

### Elves — Mana and the Recognition of Age

An elf's mana changes over life. Among people able to recognize those changes, it is the principal indication of age when adult appearance offers little guidance. Encounters can therefore have a dimension missed by a stranger relying on faces alone.

Age, magical strength and practiced skill are different qualities. Recognizing an older elven presence does not reveal all abilities, memories or personal history. Acquaintance, observation and disclosure still matter.

Old age can affect health or function despite a youthful appearance. A familiar face does not guarantee unchanged sight, mobility or endurance.

### Elvish Language and Foreign Communities

Elvish is spoken in Valcéranne and communities beyond its borders. Its use abroad reflects family, education, migration and experience. Students, traders and settled households may speak both Elvish and local languages.

A change of language can express affection, formality, irritation or familiarity. Understanding follows the actual knowledge of those present. Shared speech connects communities whose loyalties, customs and daily lives have developed differently.

### Valcéranne — Land and Kingdom

Valcéranne is a major elven kingdom of river valleys, wooded uplands, cultivated country and coastal districts. Old cities include pale stone waterfronts, steep roofs, public gardens and lively squares. Other communities live near extensive forests or the sea. Human and other foreign households live alongside its elven majority.

Long residence gives places layers of construction and memory. Gardens outlast neighboring buildings; restored homes contain work from several periods. Craftspeople, cultivators, merchants and officials continue changing that landscape.

An inland household, ambitious port and woodland community can all understand Valcéranne as home while disagreeing about its future.

### Valcéranne — Long Memory and Public Change

A bridge's original architect may still inspect repairs, and an institution's founders may remain active in its affairs. Disputes have personal continuity: living witnesses can remember both a controversial decision's promises and its cost.

Continuity supports knowledge, attachment and resistance. Projects compete with inherited commitments. Younger residents seek influence in institutions whose senior figures may have held it for centuries. Older people also adopt unfamiliar ideas, change their minds and disagree among themselves.

An elf returning after decades abroad may find familiar faces alongside relationships, loyalties and ambitions that have changed during their absence.

### Valcéranne — Study, Art and Foreign Life

Valcéranne's cities support scholarship, magical teaching, performance, craft and public pleasures. Forest communities and maritime districts have traditions shaped by their environments. Elegant architecture and manners coexist with practical work and crowded households.

Travelers carry language, books, techniques and fashions abroad. A long-lived sailor can revisit a harbor across several human generations; a scholar can correspond with a succession of people at one foreign institution. Their experiences differ from those of elves who spend most of their lives in one place.

### Dwarves — Homelands, Work and Migration

Dwarven communities include mountain settlements, maritime countries, inland towns and foreign households. Quarrying, construction, metalwork, shipbuilding, fishing and demanding trades contribute to many local histories. Families also pursue farming, scholarship, medicine, public service and the arts.

Some communities remain close to old mountain holdings. Others look outward through shipping routes and migration. A dwarven family in a distant harbor can have deep local roots alongside relatives, language and memories connected with another country.

Individuals differ in ambition, aptitude, means and interest, including how strongly they identify with inherited work.

### Dwarvish Language and Speech

Dwarvish-speaking communities preserve recognizable rhythms, expressions and varieties of speech across mountain districts, coastal settlements and foreign households. Vocabulary carries the influence of work, family life, seafaring and neighbors. Speakers can retain familiar expressions in another language.

Speech varies with region, background and company. Correspondence, a harbor argument, family conversation and public address can draw on different parts of one person's language. Dwarves raised abroad can speak with their home city's habits while knowing Dwarvish through family or study.

### Dunbrae — The Maritime Commonwealth

Dunbrae is a dwarven-majority commonwealth across a rugged mainland coast and nearby islands. Harbors, fishing villages, shipyards, quarry towns and agricultural valleys support its people. Foreign residents are especially numerous in ports where shipping brings crews, goods and news.

Member communities retain strong local governments while cooperating in maritime affairs and defense. Harbor representatives, inland landholders, working associations and families negotiate differing interests. Practical cooperation accompanies a fierce attachment to local independence.

Beyond the docks and warehouses are homes, farms, schools and small settlements whose concerns continue through the sailing seasons.

### Dunbrae — Ports, Ships and the Interior

Dunbrae's ships carry manufactured goods, materials, provisions and passengers over long routes. Its yards build and maintain working vessels, while generations of crews preserve knowledge of coasts and crossings.

The maritime economy depends on the interior and foreign exchange. Farms feed towns; roads carry goods to sea; quarries and workshops supply valuable materials. Communities debate improvements' cost and who benefits.

Returning travelers bring techniques, wages, habits and stories. Those connections support prosperous families and households whose members spend long periods away.

### Dunbrae — Company and Public Life

Launch-day gatherings, waterfront music, crew reunions and meals mark coastal life. Workmates help one another through injury, unemployment or difficult seasons, while rivalries follow them beyond the yard. Skilled repairs earn reputations; neglected jobs are remembered.

Shipowners, captains, laborers, teachers, smallholders and officials have different means and expectations. Community pride can accompany generosity, exclusion, cooperation or stubborn attachment to unsuccessful practices. Ordinary evenings remain as much a part of Dunbrae as ships and heavy work.

### Yunhai — Land and Peoples

Yunhai is a large eastern country populated by many types of beastfolk. River basins connect cultivated districts, administrative cities and coastal ports. Mountain communities and distant provinces have their own histories and traditions. Other ancestries also live and travel there.

Beastfolk bodies vary among households and individuals. Humanlike faces and builds with differing ears, horns, tails, feathers, fur or scales form much of that diversity, alongside less common forms. Regional belonging and family history matter more than a simple division by animal features.

Rivers, roads, markets and correspondence connect different livelihoods and ways of belonging to the same country.

### Yunhai — Cities, Provinces and Learning

Administrative cities, farming provinces and ports preserve histories of government, trade and development. Public offices, influential households, working communities and religious institutions contribute different kinds of authority. City life differs from that of a mountain district or a settlement tied to seasonal water.

Learning includes formal institutions, household teaching, independent masters and religious schools. Magic follows the same principles known elsewhere, taught through local terminology, exercises and habits. Agriculture, construction, medicine, craft and fieldwork give it practical expression.

Regional pride and disagreements over education, advancement and patronage create interests within Yunhai as well as abroad.

### Yunhai — Foreign Scholars and Exchange

Yunhai's scholars, artisans, traders and travelers maintain foreign connections. Overseas institutions offer teachers, facilities or opportunities, while Yunhai's schools have achievements their graduates value.

Glowhaven invites admiration, competition and criticism. Visitors may seek collaboration without accepting every foreign practice as an improvement. Letters, objects and returning travelers carry discoveries both ways.

Jianyu Wen is an independent visitor from Yunhai working at Broken Halo Ruins' conservation shelter and collaborating with Bellwind Conservatory. His existing work is one connection between distant communities, not a measure of his whole country's knowledge or interests.

### Celestials, Gods and Religious Traditions

Many religions honor beings that exist and act in the world. Ancient celestials range from recognizable angelic figures to appearances mortals find difficult to describe. Some claim to have made the world. Followers preserve sacred accounts, interpretations and memories of their deeds.

Other traditions honor nature spirits, ancestors or mythical beasts. Worship can concern a country, community or place with which a being has a longstanding relationship. It includes gratitude, hope, fear, devotion, ceremony and institutions sustained by believers.

Different traditions offer different beginnings. A being's age and power do not settle every claim made about it. Testimony passes through generations of interpretation.

### Faith, Testimony and Local Knowledge

A temple's account can combine remembered appearances, inherited teachings and commentary. Some worshippers directly experienced a being's actions; others know it through custom, communal life or distant reports. People disagree about the meaning of events they accept occurred.

Believers have different convictions and motives. A sincere adherent can question an official. A temple can provide valued assistance while holding political influence. Someone can observe a family ceremony without sharing every explanation.

Visitors learn beliefs through actual encounters. Familiar symbols acquire different associations across borders and time.

### The Oni Homeland — Country and Public Life

Beyond Glowhaven, the oni homeland has broad fields, wooded roads and distinct towns under a Shogun. Households, officials, martial schools and independent organizations share influence. Wooden streetscapes, tiled roofs, paper screens, workshops and gardens shape inhabited places, with rural life between them.

Farmers, craftspeople, couriers, scholars, performers and adventurers live alongside people in martial and administrative service. Horned ancestry dictates no allegiance or temperament. Locality, family, work and ambition give people different reasons to cooperate or quarrel.

Visiting oni retain their actual affiliations and individual lives.

### The Oni Homeland — Traditions, Workshops and Spirits

Samurai undertake learned martial, administrative or household duties. Shinobi traditions cultivate reconnaissance, concealment, fieldcraft and specialized magic through organizations with different loyalties and methods.

Selected workshops excel in Arcane- and Soul-based magitech: warded tools, spirit-message relays, assistive mechanisms and compact machinery. Expensive devices coexist with hand labor, farmland and traditional buildings. Their upkeep requires materials, power and knowledge.

Yokai, local spirits and territorial creatures inhabit the countryside. Travelers' stories mix observations, customs and exaggeration. Accounts of a torii exuding Soul magic remain accounts of an unfamiliar place, without an established route through it.

### Lives Across Borders

Countries enter ordinary life through residents, letters, imported goods and journeys. A recipe, a book in another language, a ship's arrival or a foreign-made tool can carry a connection without a diplomatic occasion.

People move for education, work, marriage, trade, belief, curiosity or necessity. Identity can encompass several places. A settled household may know its adopted town intimately while maintaining attachments elsewhere. Returning travelers find familiar relationships changed.

The world holds warm homes, hard work, celebrations, dangerous frontiers, devotion, political conflict and quiet pleasures. Different journeys encounter different parts of that range.

### Language conventions and research

Elvish remains French in dialogue. Selected French mannerisms and cityscapes accompany a broader long-lived fantasy culture. Real-world France has not been added to the map.

Represent dwarven speech with readable Scottish-accented English and selected Scots vocabulary or constructions: aye, wee, ken, dinnae, cannae. Vary strength by speaker and background. An original example: “Aye, she'll make the crossing. Whether you'll enjoy it is another matter.”

The selected effect is the recognizable accent. Scottish Gaelic was not selected as a translation language. Treatment of untranslated Dwarvish remains open. These are authoring representation conventions, kept outside public descriptive prose. No voice assets or tags change in this task.

Sources consulted:

- [Scottish Corpus of Texts & Speech — corpus details](https://www.scottishcorpus.ac.uk/corpus-details/): Scottish English, Scots and intermediate varieties, with differing regions and registers.
- [Dictionaries of the Scots Language — language and dialect](https://dsl.ac.uk/about-scots/a-language-or-a-dialect/): Scots pronunciation, grammar and vocabulary.

The readable mixture is an editorial recommendation rather than a claim that all Scots speak alike.
