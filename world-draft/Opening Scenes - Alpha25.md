## Phoenix Wing: First Day

Red pennants snap above the entrance while a broad stream of students climbs toward Phoenix Wing Academy. Returning students call to friends across the court; first-years keep glancing between the doors and the papers in their hands. Somewhere behind the practice wall, a whistle cuts through the clack of wooden blades. The breakfast stall by the steps is doing excellent business with people who insist they are not late.

The registrar's desk has been set just inside the Main Hall. A clerk checks the new student's name against the admitted list, passes over a folded timetable, and points to the waiting benches. Admission is settled. Today is about finding the right room.

Hinae Komori moves aside to make space, holding her own timetable flat with both hands.

“Morning lessons, then the afternoon's ours. I checked. I thought perhaps the blank part was a printing mistake.”

A bell sounds. The conversations travel into the hall in little waves as the first-years are called together. Up ahead, Kōhei Sanada waits beside the lectern, perfectly still beneath the red academy crest. He lets the last scrape of a bench die away before he speaks.

“Good morning. From today, you are students of Phoenix Wing Academy. You will study Magic Theory, Magical Combat, and Potions and Alchemy. You will attend the lessons on your timetables. Being talented will not excuse being absent.”

A few shoulders straighten.

“You will also learn when to stop. A duel is not improved by an unnecessary injury. A useful spell is not improved by casting it where someone is eating. We expect effort, preparation, and the sense to ask when you do not understand.”

His gaze passes over the hall without lingering on anyone.

“Your first lesson is Magic Theory, in the Lecture Hall. Haruki Saitō is expecting you after this welcome. Follow the signs through the east doors. Good luck.”

Kōhei Sanada gathers his notes and returns toward the Faculty Offices. The formal silence breaks at once into voices, bags being lifted, and somebody discovering that a timetable can be upside down. Hinae Komori studies the arrow by the east doors, then gives it a small, satisfied nod.

Through those doors, the Lecture Hall stands open. Haruki Saitō is setting a travel mug beside the board. There is still time to reach a seat before the lesson begins.

## Gryffin Tail: First Day

The cyan banners above Gryffin Tail's entrance barely move in the morning air. Everything below them is moving at once. Students with instrument cases weave around students with books; two returning classmates are arguing about a result neither has brought along. First-years gather near a desk in the Lecture Wing, where the registrar is steadily reducing a formidable stack of timetables.

The new student's name is already on the admitted list. A timetable changes hands, still faintly warm from the copying charm. Its morning blocks are filled; the afternoons are left open. Fumito Sakurai, wearing a welcome-duty ribbon over his second-year uniform, directs the newcomers toward the Lecture Hall with the concentration of someone determined not to provide three alternative routes.

“Straight ahead. The demonstrations can wait until this afternoon. That instruction is also for me.”

Inside, Nahlah Qamar has taken a place near an aisle. She turns her timetable to compare its headings with the board, then carefully frees the end of her tail from under a bench. Around her, the first-years settle into a room built for a much larger audience than any of their old classrooms.

Norio Shibata checks the hall clock and steps to the teaching floor.

“Good morning. Welcome to Gryffin Tail Academy. Your required subjects are Magic Theory, Magical Engineering, and Magical Arts. Please keep the timetable you have just received. The copy on the noticeboard is for reading, not taking.”

His delivery makes the final distinction sound thoroughly necessary.

“Lessons occupy the morning block. Afternoons are ordinarily yours for study, practice, societies, and other business. Restricted rooms remain restricted. If you find an error in a record, bring the record to the office. If you find an error in an experiment, tell the instructor before repeating it.”

He closes his folder.

“Kei Aoki will begin your first Magic Theory lesson here shortly. I will be in the Faculty Offices if an administrative matter needs correcting. I hope your first term goes well.”

The applause is brief but genuine. Norio Shibata leaves through the side door as Kei Aoki enters with a textbook under one arm and his wand in hand. The wood elf looks over the room, puts the book on the desk, and opens it to the first marked page.

“Timetables away. Something to write with.”

Chairs creak and bags open. The first question is about to go on the board.

## Dragon Hoard: First Day

Dragon Hoard's Arrival Gallery fills with yellow coats, gold-trimmed robes, luggage straps, and the smell of somebody's hastily purchased breakfast. Returning students pass the expedition exhibits without slowing. The new first-years slow down at nearly every case, until a member of staff waves them toward the timetable desk in the Exhibition Hall.

The new student's admission is already confirmed. The registrar finds the name, hands over a folded timetable, and points out the morning lesson block. Afternoons are clear unless an activity has been announced. On the next bench, Suzune Mikazuki is reading her own copy with the stern attention she usually reserves for judging whether something is tall enough to reach.

“The notices are at a sensible height this year,” she remarks. “A remarkable breakthrough.”

The first-years are called into the Lecture Hall. Beyond its windows the gallery remains busy with older students heading to their own rooms. This is the start of the academic year for everyone, not a school waiting empty for one arrival.

Miyu Asahina stands at the front with a bright smile and one small stack of notes.

“Good morning, everyone. Welcome to Dragon Hoard Academy! You have your timetables, so you already know the most important thing: where somebody expects you to be.”

A little laughter travels through the benches.

“Magic Theory, Spellcraft, and Magical Creatures are your required subjects. Your lessons are in the morning. Most afternoons are yours. Use them well, and please remember that wanting to investigate something is the beginning of a plan, rather than permission to open its case.”

She sets the notes down.

“You will learn how to ask useful questions, prepare for uncertain conditions, and bring discoveries home without losing the discovery or yourselves. First, though, you will learn the fundamentals. Noriaki Takatsuki is taking your Magic Theory lesson in this room as soon as we finish.”

Miyu Asahina looks across the first-year benches, pleased and quite serious beneath the warmth.

“Good luck. If your timetable is wrong, find me in the Faculty Offices. If you simply dislike the first bell, I sympathize. It is staying.”

She leaves the teaching floor to Noriaki Takatsuki. His spectacles catch the light as he squares a stack of paper with the desk edge. A pen rises between his fingers.

“Before we discuss what a spell can do,” he says, “we will discuss how to describe what it actually did.”

A clean diagram begins to take shape on the board. The lesson is ready to begin.

## Hydra Head: First Day

Violet awnings shade the arrivals at Hydra Head, where the opening morning has filled the court with voices. Returning friends greet each other over traveling bags. First-years compare papers, look for signs, and try to judge which of the confident people ahead of them actually knows the way. From the garden comes the scent of flowers; from Confluence Hall comes a firm request to stop blocking the timetable desk.

The new student's admission is settled. At the desk, a clerk finds the name on the first-year list and hands over the schedule. Morning lessons are printed in orderly blocks. The afternoons have room to breathe.

A call goes up for the first-year welcome. The newcomers move into Confluence Hall, where benches have been arranged facing a small lectern. Through an open side door, an older group is already heading toward its own classroom. Somewhere behind them, a decorative ribbon makes a determined attempt to accompany the wrong coat.

Kiyoko Fujinami waits until the last arrivals have found space.

“Good morning. Welcome to Hydra Head Academy. You are now members of a school where many people share rooms, equipment, and the consequences of one another's decisions. We will try to make that a pleasant experience.”

Her pause leaves just enough room for a few uncertain smiles.

“Magic Theory, Enchantment, and Herbology are required subjects. Follow the morning lessons on your timetables. Afternoons are normally free for your own studies and activities. An announced practical or examination is an exception; a rumor that a practical has been cancelled is not.”

She rests one hand on the lectern.

“You do not need to know everything today. You do need to ask rather than quietly use something you cannot identify. Respect another person's work, return shared materials, and remember that a useful disagreement can be conducted at an ordinary speaking volume.”

Kiyoko Fujinami turns slightly toward the corridor.

“Your first Magic Theory lesson is with Kana Fujimoto in the Lecture Hall. The signs lead directly there. I will return to the Faculty Offices after this welcome. Good luck with your first morning.”

As she leaves, the room fills with movement. The timetable points toward the Lecture Hall, where Kana Fujimoto has arranged a few ordinary teaching examples beside the board. She checks the clock, then looks up at the first arrivals with an eager, slightly nervous smile.

There is a place to sit, something to learn, and an afternoon waiting on the other side of the morning.

## First Day of Work: Phoenix Wing

The first-year welcome is over by the time the new instructor reaches Phoenix Wing's Faculty Offices. Outside, the morning's scattered voices are settling into the more regular sounds of lessons. Inside, Kōhei Sanada has cleared a space on his desk for an appointment letter, a teaching timetable, and a short list of rooms.

“Good morning. You must be our new instructor.”

He checks the name on the letter and indicates the chair opposite him.

“Your appointment is in Magical Combat. Ritsu Kurogane remains the course professor; you will be teaching the introductory group alongside the department. Your first supervised lesson is tomorrow, at the first bell, in the Training Yard.”

He taps that line once, then leaves the timetable where it can be read.

“Today, inspect the space and prepare a short opening exercise. Ritsu Kurogane can show you the boundaries and the practice equipment. Start with something your students can actually do. I would rather see them learn one useful thing than watch a demonstration they cannot follow.”

A sharp impact sounds outside, followed by an instructor's correction. Kōhei Sanada waits a moment, apparently satisfied by the second sound.

“The morning block is teaching time. Most afternoons are available for preparation, consultations, and your own work, unless a duty has been arranged. If a student is injured, stop the exercise. If you need different equipment, ask before the lesson.”

His gaze returns to the new instructor, direct rather than unfriendly.

“You have the rest of today to get ready. What would you like clarified before you see the yard?”

## First Day of Work: Gryffin Tail

Norio Shibata has the new instructor's folder ready before the knock at his office door. The first lessons of the year are underway beyond the Lecture Wing; here in the Faculty Offices, a kettle hums beside a gray tray of completed forms.

“Come in. New instructor? Good morning.”

He compares the appointment letter with the folder, then turns the teaching timetable around.

“Magical Engineering. Sōichirō Takamine leads the subject. You are taking an introductory group in the Research Rooms at the first bell tomorrow. You are an additional instructor, not his replacement.”

The explanation is delivered without ceremony, as though preventing that particular misunderstanding has become part of the job.

“Today, find the room, check the available teaching apparatus, and decide on your first exercise. Sōichirō Takamine can answer questions about the course materials. Please arrange access to anything restricted instead of discovering that the cabinet is locked while a class waits.”

Norio Shibata draws a small pencil mark beside the room name.

“Morning lessons follow the timetable. Preparation and consultations usually fit in the afternoon, apart from separately arranged duties. If this room assignment is wrong for the equipment you need, tell me today. I can correct a timetable. I cannot make two classes fit through the same door.”

He sets the pencil down and opens his notebook to a clean line.

“Tomorrow, first bell. Research Rooms. Is there anything you need before you begin preparing?”

## First Day of Work: Dragon Hoard

The door to Dragon Hoard's Faculty Offices is open, and Miyu Asahina is directing a procession of paper slips into three trays. She catches one before it joins the wrong stack, places it by hand, and looks up at the new instructor with a bright smile.

“Welcome! Yes, that's your appointment letter. Wonderful. You're the person I was expecting.”

She brings the teaching timetable onto a clear part of the desk.

“You're joining Magical Creatures. Genzō Iwakura leads the subject, and you'll be taking an introductory observation lesson tomorrow at the first bell, in the Discovery Labs. Start with the teaching specimens and records. Nothing needs to be released, chased, or brought back from a mountain today.”

The smile does not diminish when she says it.

“Today, go through the room with Genzō Iwakura, check what you can use, and prepare one manageable exercise. We want students to notice something accurately before they become wonderfully confident about noticing everything.”

She traces the morning block on the timetable. The afternoon space beneath it is mercifully clear.

“Your normal teaching sits here. Preparation and consultations can fit around it, and we'll agree on any extra duties separately. You do not need to volunteer for an expedition to make a good first impression.”

A slip of paper drifts toward her elbow. She pins it under the empty tray without looking away.

“First bell tomorrow. Discovery Labs. One useful lesson. Does that give you what you need, or shall we sort something out before you meet the department?”

## First Day of Work: Hydra Head

A quiet knock brings an answer from Hydra Head's Faculty Offices. Kiyoko Fujinami has set an appointment book beside a small tray of tea. Beyond the window, students cross the Garden Walk on their way to morning lessons; the office itself is calm enough to hear the cups settle.

“Good morning. Our new instructor, I presume. Please come in.”

She reads the appointment letter, checks one entry, and places the teaching timetable within easy reach.

“You are joining Enchantment. Reon Fujisaki leads the subject. Your first introductory session is tomorrow at the first bell, in the Seminar Room. He knows to expect you today.”

A small pause follows.

“He may call you darling. It is not an administrative title.”

Kiyoko Fujinami turns the timetable to show the morning block.

“Today, look over the room and course materials, then prepare your opening exercise. Make sure the students can distinguish a stable effect from a decorative one. If an example needs special materials, arrange them before the lesson. A beautiful explanation becomes less useful when the apparatus is still in somebody's private cupboard.”

She offers the tea without pushing it nearer.

“Most teaching takes place in the morning. Afternoons usually leave room for preparation and consultations, apart from agreed duties. We can adjust practical details now more easily than tomorrow, with a class waiting.”

Kiyoko Fujinami closes the appointment book, leaving the timetable open.

“What would you like to ask before you see the Seminar Room?”
