## Shared musical identity

Working concept: a city whose ordinary days are worth caring about. Intelligent, warm and rhythmically alive, with ambition, awkwardness, friendship, curiosity and room for mistakes. School life should have songs people want to hear again.

The everyday ensemble centers on piano/Rhodes, rounded electric or upright bass, drums with a relaxed hip-hop pocket, flute/clarinet, selected clean guitar, and a distinctive short chime sound. Modern score instruments are musical choices, not new in-world technology or canon.

This ensemble is a home base. Research may become eccentric electronic pop; emotional scenes may use intimate alternative R&B or rock; combat may add sharp guitars, breakbeats and stronger drums; wonder may become experimental fantasy-pop. Each change should serve the scene while retaining a few audible connections to the rest of the album.

## Make the theme before making the catalogue

1. Produce CampusDay - Before the Bell first. Choose a take for its actual melody, bass groove, vocal phrasing and emotional character.
2. Preserve the approved recording and, where available, its accompaniment/stems or a simple melodic transcription.
3. Identify a short original melodic hook, a recognizable bass/rhythm gesture and one chime timbre. These become shared reference material.
4. Build Lecture, DiningHall, AfterClass, Study and Evening as rearrangements informed by that audio. They can change tempo, harmony, singer and instrumentation.
5. Listen for an audible family resemblance. Repeating the same adjectives in six unrelated prompts is not evidence that the resulting music shares a theme.
6. Only then expand into city life, guild work, exploration, emotional scenes and combat.

Suno's current [v6 FAQ](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/13924481) describes reference inputs and revisions using existing musical material. Use the tools actually available in the account. A reference helps communicate intent; the generated result still needs listening review.

## Three recurring musical connections

These are composition proposals, not claims about already finished music:

- The curiosity hook: a short original phrase introduced by the campus song. Lecture spaces out its notes, Study stretches its answer, and wonder can broaden its intervals and harmony.
- The forward-motion groove: a small syncopated bass pickup and drum placement. Dining turns it conversational, AfterClass makes it buoyant, and ordinary combat can sharpen its attacks.
- The evening answer: a quieter or reharmonized response to the campus hook. It can return in friendship, recovery and reflection without requiring every emotional song to be sad.

## Voices and dialogue versions

Original English vocals are the creative default. Vary the singer's range, phrasing and temperament by song: smooth rhythmic singing, a playful conversational lead, a fragile intimate voice, or an expressive rock delivery where appropriate. A shared production identity does not require one singer or constant belting.

Keep the full songs worth listening to outside the game. Write lyrics around portable feelings and images rather than reciting a quest, NPC biography or game-state fact.

For gameplay:
- The full vocal recording can serve as the main/gameplay version.
- A companion narration mix should preserve the same musical identity while reducing or removing lead vocals for spoken scenes.
- Lecture and Study especially benefit from an instrumental narration arrangement. If only one version exists initially, audition the vocal density carefully before using it under extended speech.
- Obtain the alternate from the actual production or appropriate separation/editing tools; a new generation with the same text is not automatically an instrumental version of the same recording.

Voyage supports separate narration/gameplay audio within a variant. Its field help says narration audio is also preferred when switching playlists during gameplay, so the narration version should have a useful entrance too. See the [music reference](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/other/gameplayMusicSettings).

Suno documents paid-plan [stem separation](https://help.suno.com/en/articles/12702337); availability and separation quality must be checked for the actual account and recording.

## Prompt and review rules

Each initial prompt below is one paragraph under 1,000 characters. Tempo is an artistic target, not a guaranteed output or a Voyage field. The first prompt is standalone; the later prompts explicitly expect the approved campus recording as a supplied reference.

Judge each result on:
- A hook that survives repeated listening.
- Its complete scene mood, including the bridge and ending.
- Its relationship to the approved anchor.
- Vocal character and intelligibility.
- Space under spoken narration.
- Consistent perceived loudness and manageable dynamics.
- Its actual transition/loop, checked by listening.

A song can be beautiful and still fail its intended gameplay use. Keep successful takes for a more suitable family instead of forcing every result into the requested slot.

## First six generation briefs

### CampusDay - Before the Bell

**Proposed scene/mood tags:** academy life; campus; school day; class change; students.

**Direction:** The first anchor song: warm jazz and hip-hop with a memorable original vocal hook.

**Why it fits:** Establishes the daily-life identity and a motif that the next five cues can rearrange.

**Suno Simple Mode prompt (822 characters):**

Original English-language jazz-pop and mellow hip-hop song for daily life at a magical academy, around 96 BPM with a relaxed swung pocket. Warm Rhodes ninth chords, rounded electric bass with an anticipatory pickup, tight soft drums, conversational clarinet and a small bell-like chime answer. A smooth, charismatic alto moves between lightly rhythmic verses and a clear singable chorus about unfinished plans, familiar faces and choosing what comes next. Introduce a distinctive short original melodic hook, returning in voice, piano and chimes. Give the second verse a fresh bass variation and the bridge a brief reflective turn, then restore the same welcoming mood. Leave generous instrumental space; warm clear production, no giant drop or grand finale. Original melody and lyrics; loop-compatible ending, clean loop.

### Lecture - Notes in the Margin

**Proposed scene/mood tags:** lecture; classroom; lesson; magic theory; instruction.

**Direction:** A sparse, thoughtful rearrangement of the approved CampusDay anchor.

**Why it fits:** Keeps curiosity and gentle forward movement while reducing the social energy and vocal density of the campus track.

**Suno Simple Mode prompt (825 characters):**

Use the supplied original campus-theme reference to make a restrained English-language jazz and downtempo song for classroom learning, around 80 BPM. Retain its recognizable melodic shape and warm keyboard color, spacing the hook between soft piano voicings, a rounded bass line, quiet brushed drums and short flute answers. An intimate tenor sings concise verses about questions, crossed-out answers and understanding arriving slowly; a modest chorus opens the harmony without raising the volume. Include long instrumental passages and an understated final return of the reference motif. Curious, attentive and lightly charming, with precise rhythm and a clean close mix. Avoid literal lectures in the lyrics, sleepy ambient drift, dramatic builds and dense lead singing. Original lyrics; loop-compatible ending, clean loop.

A relaxed mezzo sings about saving a place, trading small stories and letting the day breathe, with brief call-and-response backing lines in the chorus. Rephrase the reference hook over new chord colors rather than repeating its arrangement. A short instrumental conversation leads back into a compact chorus. Original lyrics; loop-compatible ending, clean loop.
