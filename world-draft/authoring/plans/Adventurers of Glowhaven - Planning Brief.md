# Adventurers of Glowhaven — planning brief

Planning edition 1.0 · 31 October 2026. This document records confirmed scope and proposed designs. It does not implement changes in the world JSON.

**Historical plan:** implemented 1 November 2026. The [release development notes](../../Adventurers%20of%20Glowhaven%20-%20Development%20Notes.md) describe the actual shipped configuration and remaining live tests; use those for current behavior. Other expansion plans remain outside this release.

## Confirmed scope

- Three native narrative arcs: a Drakenspire snow-spirit investigation, a senior student's Asterveil research expedition, and Asterveil loot thieves.
- Fourteen to eighteen new NPCs. Their independent identities, relationships and ordinary lives matter beyond their arcs.
- The snow spirit is intelligent and dangerous. Fighting or negotiation can resolve the problem.
- The researcher is an adult fourth-year **Hydra Head Academy** student. Her rare item exists; her theory is partly wrong.
- The thieves are mostly comic bullies and opportunists. Their actions can still hurt other adventurers.
- These are midgame stories, approximately levels 25–50. Orrum retains his current level 68 and need not be fought.
- Ragnar Leonhart, Adoha Aonuma and Karia Morino receive cross-realm routines. Adoha's Asterveil destinations stay within the Canopy settlement.
- Ragnar's intended level is **67**. The earlier review JSON's 90 must not overwrite the user's change.
- More quests in Glowhaven, Drakenspire, Asterveil and Wraith Island Prison; no Silken Descent expansion in this release.
- Additional monster types and broader starting equipment choices.

The existing guild ladder, licensure, service credits and court portal permissions remain the foundation. Posted rewards are paid in full. This plan supersedes the smaller eight-person adventurer proposal in the earlier seven-update blueprint.

| Component | Proposed size |

Arc-generated chapters are not included in the sixteen standalone quests. Their exact number depends on runtime progression; roughly three meaningful developments per arc is a pacing aim, not a compulsory checklist.

## Arc proposal: The Woman Beyond the Frost

**Hook:** a competent martial adventuring party has accepted a commission to investigate dangerous cold and attacks near Drakenspire's outskirts. At Coldglass Galleries, ordinary tracking stops being enough. They need someone who can distinguish a creature's passage from residual mana and recognize what is directing the cold.

The party can appear first at Whitepass Caravan Lodge and later use a camp area at the existing cavern location. Foreground two speakers during introductions; the whole group need not speak in rotation.

**Underlying conflict — proposed:** an intelligent snow spirit has claimed a winter passage as her domain. Orrum has repeatedly broken her ice barriers to keep his own route open. She treats this as humiliation and retaliates, allowing her cold and lesser spirits to threaten lower routes used by people. Her grievance is genuine, but she chooses disproportionate retaliation. She is capable of courtesy, bargaining and deliberate cruelty; she is not automatically innocent because she is a spirit.

Give her ordinary tastes and vanity: an exacting hostess who treasures beautifully made objects and holds grudges over etiquette. Those details should shape encounters without becoming an instruction to placate her with a particular gift.

**Development:** examine the cold's effects and the crew's first findings; distinguish deliberate barriers from natural cave hazards; discover the territorial conflict; confront the spirit with a credible plan, force, or both. Ordinary enemies and terrain create work before the main confrontation. Arrival at the cavern does not reveal everything.

**Viable endings:**

- Defeat or banish her, ending her ability to threaten the lower routes.
- Broker a separation of territories and actually establish the agreed safe passage.
- Drive her away or break the mechanism sustaining her attacks, with a clear account of where the danger now stands.
- Make a selfish bargain that protects the client's route while knowingly favoring her interests. The consequences concern the affected people rather than universal faction hostility.
- Withdraw or abandon the work. This is not successful completion merely because the party learned her name.

**Expected conclusion:** the attacks on the commissioned route have actually ceased through a consequential outcome, with the spirit's fate, the passage's condition, the crew's relationships and any obligations preserved.

Orrum remains elusive. His tracks, broken barriers and remembered behavior can establish his involvement. Negotiation must not require discovering his shelter or turning him into a dependable quest contact. He can meet the player when circumstances justify it, without becoming a scheduled tavern guest.

The commission should concern safety and investigation, leaving room for a nonlethal settlement. A murder-only contract would undermine the proposed choices. Tentative spirit power is around the high end of the arc's band, calibrated against her actual tier and group encounter rather than level alone.

**Access for different playstyles:** a nonmage can contribute through tracking, climbing, practical experiments, protecting a specialist, interviewing witnesses or hiring qualified help. A borrowed instrument needs an actual owner and limitations. Lack of the right discipline changes the approach; it should not make the entire story unavailable.

## Arc proposal: A Thesis No One Would Fund

**Hook:** a fourth-year Hydra Head student has spent much of her savings posting an expedition request. No adventurers accepted: the destination is dangerous, and they regard her target as an embellished local tale. She can be met at Asterveil Canopy with a battered specimen case and a notebook full of highly specific objections to the dismissals.

**Character direction:** a shy, green-haired, bespectacled researcher in the broad Sucrose vein, adapted as an original person. She is animated and unexpectedly stubborn about specimens, physically unimpressive, and capable of a bad decision when excitement gets ahead of caution. She also has friendships, a hobby, opinions about her faculty, and pleasures unrelated to finishing the thesis. Her quiet manner does not make her incapable of disagreement or a secret combat prodigy.

**Working discovery: the Stillwind Pearl.** A rare biological accretion associated with an elusive sky creature. She believes it stores enough stable mana to explain persistent island levitation. It actually records a short-lived pattern of local air and mana currents: scientifically valuable and useful to navigation research, but not a perpetual power supply. The precise creature, anatomy and limitations remain proposals.

Her research is therefore worthwhile without being completely correct. Local fae knowledge should offer real evidence rather than being automatically dismissed as superstition or secretly containing every answer.

**Development:** compare testimony and old samples; follow evidence into an appropriate Asterveil region; locate and study a real source; decide how to obtain evidence without automatically destroying the source. More than one route should be credible. Avoid four compulsory regional errands just to touch every map quadrant.

**Viable endings:** recover the intact object; obtain a limited sample and persuasive field evidence; study it in place with local cooperation; appropriate or sell it and face the researcher's reaction; or conclude the attempt honestly after a real setback. A promise to return later does not equal successful research.

**Expected conclusion:** the existence and real properties of the object are established, the expedition's actual outcome is settled, and the researcher must decide what her corrected thesis will claim. A completed expedition does not automatically graduate her or count as the player's own academic thesis.

She can accompany a willing party, hire another helper, or remain at a safe staging area while the player investigates. Companion membership needs agreement. Her vulnerability should support interesting preparation and choices, without forcing constant escort micromanagement or punishing the player for talking to someone else.

## Arc proposal: A Very Reasonable Recovery Fee

**Hook:** a four-person adventuring crew has decided that tired expeditioners are easier to rob than monsters are to fight. They invent route tolls, offer unsolicited “protection,” claim freshly won loot, or insist that standing nearby counts as helping with the kill.

Their party chemistry is comic: a self-important leader, a showy bruiser who hates inconvenient terrain, a fast-talking appraiser who starts valuing goods while the owner still holds them, and a lookout who repeatedly punctures the group's grandiose claims. All four still need individual wants, skills and relationships beyond the joke.

They are opportunists, not automatic murderers. They pick favorable situations, retreat from evident superior force, argue over their own shares and may be bargained with. They are not secretly righteous, and their comedy does not erase actual harm.

**Development:** hear or witness a specific incident; establish who took what and where the crew operates; choose whether to confront them, expose them, outwit them, arrange a sanctioned challenge or exploit their greed.

**Viable endings:** recover stolen property and break the operation; defeat and hand over the actual offenders; secure an enforceable settlement; buy them off; or cooperate with them and inherit particular enemies and obligations. Killing them is possible without plot resurrection or mandatory replacement lookalikes.

**Expected conclusion:** the particular extortion operation and claims over the affected property are resolved, with the surviving people and resulting relationships preserved. Unresolved resentment can support later encounters; it does not reopen the completed quest automatically.

The first incident should normally involve an authored victim so the player can understand the crew before risking their own possessions. Actual player theft requires a successful resolved action and an actual inventory change. Never remove equipment just to establish that the villains are threatening. Their approaches need not occur on every Asterveil visit, and cleared camps do not refill instantly.

## Cast plan: sixteen independent people

| Group | Count | Independent characterization to develop |
|---|---:|---|
| Cave-investigation adventurers | 4 | A boastful captain who writes terrible poetry; a fashion-conscious shield bearer; a crossbow user hoping for stage work; a practical field medic with a blunt interest in money |
| Snow spirit | 1 | Territorial pride, exacting hospitality, collecting beautiful objects, her own history beyond this dispute |
| Hydra Head researcher | 1 | An ordinary friendship, embarrassing hobby, academic disagreement and habits beyond specimen hunting |
| Opportunistic thieves | 4 | Different ambitions, tastes, loyalties and limits; friendship does not erase selfishness |
| Guild regulars | 4 | A career Bronze courier, a solo monster hunter, a magitech salvage specialist, and a sociable combat healer |
| Clients or supporting residents | 2 | A local Drakenspire route stakeholder and an Asterveil expedition victim, each with work and relationships beyond the incident |

These are provisional role sketches. Names, species, ages, affiliations, precise weapons and affinity draws come during character-card design using the existing format. Vary the cast; do not make all sixteen secretly overworked, selfless, romantically available, or latent geniuses.

Reuse Komugi Hanabishi, Natsume Hibari, Yuzuri Takase, Kureha Kazahana and other established locals where their actual knowledge and availability fit. New cast members must have something to do after their associated story is over. Small adventuring parties do not require a new company-management system.

## Cross-realm routines

Use the existing native placement approach demonstrated by Orivelle. A six-day touring circuit is a proposed route cycle, not new lore declaring that the world has a six-day week. Give the three characters different offsets and stops so they do not travel as an unexplained permanent trio.

| NPC | Glowhaven | Drakenspire | Asterveil |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ragnar Leonhart | Crucible practice or observation; guild visits | Stonefang Training Grounds, a weaponsmith, Whitepass Caravan Lodge | Canopy rest and selected wilderness excursions |
| Adoha Aonuma | Four Sages Bazaar, inn or teahouse | Snowbell Playhouse, The Copper Ram | Asterveil Canopy's guest terrace and settlement areas only |
| Karia Morino | Guild training and relaxed city visits | Whitepass Caravan Lodge, The Cinder Pit | Canopy and selected field shelters or wilderness routes |

Ragnar stays level 67. His horse remains lore; no horse NPC is added. Adoha does not become a wandering wilderness combat encounter because Sound magic exists on her card. Karia's outings fit her actual training and preferences.

Movement pauses separately for each NPC during scene presence, combat, party membership, captivity, incapacitation or an actual ongoing commitment. Dead characters do not resume tours. Release or departure permits the next appropriate route step rather than an abrupt mid-conversation relocation. Record the last applied route block per NPC to avoid repeated movement.

Cross-realm travel uses their established touring/work authorization and public portal network in the fiction. It does not grant the player a key or move the player's party. Exact destination area keys are checked during implementation. Test a day transition, agreeing to accompany the player, dismissal, injury and capture.

The user's manually adjusted voices are preserved. The three review cards are not yet in the active roster; merge their latest approved forms before attaching schedules.

## More work throughout the existing realms

Proposed standalone distribution: **six Glowhaven, five Drakenspire, four Asterveil and one Wraith Island Prison**. This is a starting allocation, not sixteen reskinned errands.

- Glowhaven: contain a faulty enchanted commission; investigate missing goods; clear a dangerous service route; settle a bounded salvage dispute; protect a fragile consignment; support a practical research trial.
- Drakenspire: rescue trapped mine workers; investigate stolen sacred offerings; recover useful material from abandoned machinery; make a caravan route usable; handle a specific wyvern problem without treating sacred dragons as pests.
- Asterveil: track a particular dangerous creature; repair a field instrument in hostile terrain; recover an expeditioner's cache; investigate a disturbed pollination route.
- Wraith Island Prison: a lawful, supervised repair or supply contract for an authorized worker or participating prisoner. It grants no automatic escape route, custody exemption, portal key or sentence reduction.

Check these against existing contracts before choosing final premises. Prefer a few independent scenes with named people, practical obstacles and alternative methods. Most of these jobs conclude at their agreed scope; they do not all reveal another villain. Preserve the existing distinction between expedition work and academy field tests.

## Monster additions

Target about six genuinely distinct types after checking the current ninety-six. Existing Crystal Moths, Chimeback Striders, Veilhook Rays and similar creatures already cover several tempting ideas and should be reused where appropriate.

Possible additions:

- **Rimewake Burrower:** a segmented creature swimming under packed snow; a traveling ridge and hollow crunch warn of its approach.
- **Cairnback Hoardmarten:** a heavy mountain scavenger carrying warm mineral nodules in its back fur; aggressively protective of its cache.
- **Mirror-Gullet:** an amphibious Asterveil predator whose translucent throat projects misleading reflections around a real ambush point.
- **Spindleclaw Weaver:** a climbing island creature that anchors fibrous bridges between exposed surfaces and detects disturbances along them.
- **Compass Thorn Colony:** mobile burrlike organisms whose coordinated pull disrupts metallic equipment and loose footing near their colony.
- **Sootlace Gremlin:** a small urban magical pest that feeds on active enchantment residue, leaving curled black filament around faulty fittings.

Names and visuals remain proposals. Each type needs a diagnostic silhouette, a habitat, behavior, warning signs, a credible combat or avoidance approach, and useful material only when its actual biology supports it. Groups become actual encounter actors through the supported generation flow; a type entry alone does not populate a location. The named snow spirit is one persistent NPC, not a recurring random boss type.

## Expanded starting equipment

The current eleven choices include catalysts, a blunted Practice Blade, a Training Shield and practical kits. They offer limited ready adventurer weapon variety.

Propose about ten additional modest loadouts: sword and buckler, spear, bow and arrows, crossbow and bolts, paired short blades, two-handed sword, axe, mace and shield, polearm, and a basic spellblade weapon with a suitable focus. Reuse existing supported skill families instead of adding a skill for every weapon.

Keep one starting equipment selection. Separate training and affinity choices continue to determine competence. A weapon does not supply spells or an automatic element. Ammunition, handedness and item slots need compatible starter records; no silently infinite bolts or extra equipment grants.

A purposeful revision to consider: retain training equipment as an explicit practice choice while allowing a usable ordinary adventurer's blade. An adult martial start should not be forced to face lethal enemies with the only available sword defined as blunt. Equivalent kits should have comparable practical value; large kits do not receive extra numerical bonuses merely because they contain more objects.

## Voyage implementation approach

The supplied Luxcellia export uses authored quests and triggers; it has no native arc entries. Borrow its character-driven briefs, staged revelations and meaningful outcomes, while testing native arcs as a new feature here.

The community [arc reference](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/world/arcs) documents premise-led surfacing and runtime quest association. It does not expose an authorable quest `arcId`. Define three arcs; test their surfacing, linkage and resolution before promising chapter labels. Expected conclusions describe outcomes, not mandatory player choices.

Use native detailed quests for standalone contracts, with typed completion conditions and one reward owner. The community [quest reference](https://wiki.unofficial.voyage/world/quests) documents concluding quests and follow-up effects. Arc-generated chapters must not duplicate a prewritten quest with the same objective. Keep fixed client commissions separate from developing discoveries where appropriate.

Official [patch 2.46.4](https://voyage.io/whats-new/patch/patch%3A100002348) describes a storyline becoming dormant after its final active quest is abandoned. Test abandonment and later rediscovery; do not deliberately keep dragging a declined storyline back into unrelated scenes.

Triggers own exact outcomes that need them: scheduled placement, a real unique item award, resolved hostility or a specific permanent consequence. Use existing guild settlement and credit ownership. Ordinary acceptance, conversations and each cave corridor do not need parallel custom trackers. Public descriptions stay free of instructions about flags, generation, authored content or saves.

## Build sequence and tests

1. Lock the story premises and independent cast. Review full NPC JSON cards before portraits and the world merge.
2. Preserve the latest world and latest approved reference cards, including the user's images, voice choices and Ragnar's level 67.
3. Implement a small native-arc pilot using the researcher premise; test surfacing, a generated associated quest, abandonment and a concluding outcome. Inspect actual runtime labels before expanding to all three.
4. Add the other two arcs, reusable types, local encounter guidance and varied finite contracts.
5. Attach the three roaming schedules with pause/resume tests. Add the selected starting loadouts and verify creation grants once.
6. Test a nonmage investigation route; negotiated and violent spirit resolutions; honest corrected research; bullying without unavoidable player inventory loss; and a genuine failed or abandoned undertaking.
7. Verify real actor placement, actual stolen-property ownership, one payment, personal guild credit, no implicit portal permission, and no duplicate character creation. Include a two-player reward and travel check.

No large public-rumor system, housing economy or comprehensive item-refinement overhaul is bundled into this plan. Those remain separate proposals. A full export, changed-entity list, test evidence and concise public patch notes belong to the later implementation delivery.
