# Schools of magic, Rebirth!

## The Wizard of Shadowspire

Planning brief · 4 November 2026

**Release scope.** Feature the new reincarnated opening first in Story Starts and in the announcement. Build the Wizard of Shadowspire as its long-form storyline. Expand the general lore and target about 100 additional scrolls. The author is adding a trading port outside Glowhaven beside Brookmeadow. It gives the player somewhere to work, shop and spend time between chapters. This pass drafts the content and leaves it unapplied to WorldAlpha.

## The featured start

**Proposed menu title** · Rebirth — A Morning in Brookmeadow

**Menu description** · You wake up in a body that isn’t yours, with a whole other life still in your memory. The village of Brookmeadow has a bed, a hot breakfast, and a neighbor who’s late for work. For now, that’s enough.

> You wake up in a body that isn’t yours.
>
> The voice that comes out when you clear your throat is wrong. There’s a basin on the stool beside the bed. The face in the water moves when you do. You still remember the room where you last went to sleep, down to the view from its window.
>
> “If you can sit up, there’s broth.”
>
> The woman at the stove sets a bowl on the table. A rag is folded under its rim to stop it rocking.
>
> “We found you where the South Road meets Mill Lane. Kenta thought you were dead. He’s been out there practicing how to apologize in case you heard him.”
>
> From outside comes a short, indignant “I was checking if they could hear me.”
>
> She lifts the latch. In the yard, Kenta Matsuba is sharing a pear with a cart horse. Farther down the lane a mill wheel turns. A sliver of blue light travels down the iron hinge of the stove, and the woman nudges it back into place with a poker.
>
> “You can have breakfast before you tell me what to call you,” she says. “Or the other way round.”

The opening ends before the player answers. The bowl and the offered name give them something immediate to respond to. They can ask where they are, test the new body, explain or conceal their memories, or walk out to meet Kenta. The narration should adapt the bodily cues to the chosen character. The last remembered room in the sample is a replaceable detail. The player owns their name, ancestry, appearance, past-life particulars and response. Keep the previous occupant’s fate open until the author decides what happened.

**Opening circle.** Stage the recovery in a cottage off **Brookmeadow - Mill Lane**. This is a proposed scene within an existing area. Use one new recurring cottage resident as the practical caregiver. She wants her spare room back before the next market day and has a batch of lamp covers to finish. Kenta already works around **Brookmeadow - South Road**. His habit of making promises on the spot and his desire for a paid music booking give him reasons to check in and reasons to leave. **Haruho Taneda** already works on Mill Lane and can be the first recurring work contact. Bring him in after the player steps outside or asks about work. **Shiori Kawase** can reappear later at **Pinewater River - Brookmeadow Ford**, where she has her own work and judgment. Establish why each person is present. Keep help, friendship, lodging arrangements, lessons and recruitment voluntary.

**Summoned one** · “You woke in another body with the memories of your previous life intact. So far, you alone know what happened to you.”

Use this as a narrative origin with no proposed stat package, automatic magical mastery or public allegiance. Story-start trait eligibility controls which choices are shown. Prototype a one-shot mechanical grant at tick zero, gated by the exact `story-start` and guarded against an existing trait. The start prose supplies the opening premise. Verify the grant timing and visibility in the target Studio build. Test the lobby, player sheet, public narration, NPC dialogue, quest journal and a later join. Keep the new opening separate from the existing Brookmeadow village-life and summoned-familiar starts. First in the list is the requested promotion. Whether to change the current default selection is open. Start in solo for the prototype. Shared play needs an explicit rule for who carries the origin and how the story recognizes them. ## The story across a long save

### Act I · A life in Brookmeadow

**1. The borrowed morning.** The question is whether the player wants to stay. Offer a meal, a place to sleep for the night and two people they can choose to know. Let them walk out, pick up ordinary work, look for a teacher or take the road to Glowhaven. Haruho can offer an honest introduction to the mill. A local lesson can show that a spell takes practice and help even when a person remembers a whole previous life. Keep this first stretch entirely about making a place for oneself. No royal agent, ritual signature, pursuit, surveillance or ominous messenger enters the opening or its early follow-ups.

**2. Something worth coming back for.** Give the quiet stretch a beginning, problem and payoff of its own. A suggested episode is a market delivery on a bad river day. The mill owes a waterside cook a load of flour, Shiori closes the unsafe crossing, and Kenta has promised to arrive in time for his first chance at a paid set. The player can find a safe route, help renegotiate the delivery, support Kenta separately or make other plans. A later meal or return visit pays off what the player actually did. A player who leaves Brookmeadow can build an equivalent foothold elsewhere and meet the wider story through that life. The episode is an optional way to earn a connection. Its resolution works as a satisfying stopping point.

### Act II · The old experiment

**3. An inquiry without a name.** Bring the larger plot into play after the opening has had time to breathe and the player has moved into established play. An Ardelis delegation renews its request for military cooperation. The city’s public refusal is the first piece of political context. Separately, the court’s researchers reopen their first experiment because their second series of tests can now read a damaged relay plate. It narrows the old misfire to a stretch of countryside near Brookmeadow. This is a proposed reason for the delay. The record points to a place and time, with no name, face, or reliable way to identify a soul. The player might come across a survey request through a trusted local or a dispute over that request in Glowhaven. They can investigate or go about their life. This chapter can end with the player learning what Ardelis attempted while the court still has no personal lead.

**4. The Wizard of Shadowspire.** A copied fragment of the survey shows a symbol **Valkazathoth'dmir**, called **Vladimir**, recognizes as part of his own diagram. He can respond to a credible contact by correcting the drawing, arranging a meeting at **Shadowspire - Outer Clearing**, or offering to teach the player how to recognize similar workings. He learns who the player is through what they show or tell him. They can work with him, seek a civic or scholarly route to understand the fragment, or decline involvement. Give the chapter an answer the player can take away. Ardelis tried to bring in a person, and Vladimir wrote the original diagram before he left court. His precise reasons for drawing it and the fate of the original body stay open. The tower itself can remain sealed.

**5. The paladin detail.** This is the earliest targeted hunt, in the latter part of the middle act. The court needs a credible link from the failed invocation to a particular person and a usable lead to where they are. A proposed example is a witness report of an unusual arrival at the right place and time, corroborated by information the player later discloses to someone who actually passes it on. A court agent can pursue a mistaken lead and lose it. Ordinary casting, leveling or simply carrying the trait supplies none of that evidence. The prospective combat also needs its own level-readiness check. If the court never identifies the player, this chapter can play as a political attempt to obtain records or fall away entirely. If a detail arrives, give the player warning and let them fight, evade, seek protection, discredit the warrant or end up in actual custody. Escape, release and the absence of any pursuit carry forward as distinct outcomes.

### Act III · The weapon they wanted

**6. The second arrival.** Ardelis stabilizes the ritual and adds command magic regardless of the inquiry’s result. It sends a stronger, eerily methodical reincarnate into Glowhaven as a weapons test. A proposed scene opens near a city gate with a specific objective. The rival has been ordered to keep a breach open long enough to measure the city’s response. They concentrate on that task as the fighting spills into a street used by civilians. The player can evacuate people, interfere with the command focus, confront the rival or collect evidence. A player arriving afterward can deal with witnesses, damage and the political consequences of an attack that has actually taken place in the save. The event frames the emergency. Its outcome depends on play.

**7. The choice that was yours.** The question from the cottage returns in a different form. If the second arrival survives and is freed, the player can give them time and ask what they want to be called, echoing the offered name in Brookmeadow. Their response belongs to them. They might ask for shelter, insist on leaving, or blame the player for how the crisis ended. If they are still under command, the player can pursue the chain of command or help Glowhaven secure protection. If they die, witnesses and evidence carry the political resolution, and the person is remembered through what the player actually learned. An optional return to a familiar table or to Kenta’s next performance shows what became of the life the player chose. Close the field test and local consequences here. The wider war can remain unresolved.

### Pacing and who can know what

| Point in the story | Ardelis can know | The player can learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening and ordinary life | Its invocation failed to deliver anyone. | Their memories and new circumstances. The village knows only what it observed when it found them. |
| Later diplomatic pressure and survey | The misfire left a regional trace. | Ardelis is looking into an old incident. The player can suspect it concerns them before the court does. |
| Shadowspire and a possible corroborated lead | An agent may have grounds to suspect a particular person. | The original diagram and Vladimir’s connection. He can infer displacement while the player’s memories remain theirs to share. |
| Optional pursuit and second arrival | A real sighting or credible report may justify sending the detail. The second experiment can succeed independently. | The extent of Ardelis’s program and what the rival is able to reveal. |

Keep the shift from regional inquiry to personal hunt visible in play, with separate scenes for the first lead and any pursuit. Readiness for the inquiry comes from an established chapter and a plausible contact, with a route for players who left the village. Level readiness applies to a paladin confrontation. If a player delays the story, reopening a chapter should respect the last established facts. An unstarted inquiry can wait, a false lead can cool, and real aftermath can persist. Keep knowledge attached to the people who actually received it.

**The diplomatic dispute.** One proposed pressure point is an Ardelis demand to move a military expedition through Glowhaven’s portal network. Glowhaven refuses access. When the inquiry surfaces, the city can dispute Ardelis’s claimed right to detain someone on its soil. A stamped order may prove the paladins had instructions to seize a person. Separate ritual evidence would be needed to prove the court commissioned the experiment. The exact war and refusal terms are open.

**The breakthrough.** A proposed explanation is that the Ardelis team reconstructs a missing stabilizing line from its own failed experiments, then adds a separate command seal during the second invocation. Capture may improve their countermeasures or strengthen a lead. Soul extraction is their objective and a danger in a custody scene. Give custody an authored exit through escape, exchange or intervention from contacts the player actually earned. Treat capture or extraction as distinct outcomes that require their own evidence. Preserve each named paladin’s actual condition and whereabouts.

**The rival.** Their baseline disposition is neutral. They come from an alternate Earth without magic. The link to the player’s original world is open. Give the rival a stable identity and a habit that survives control. For example, when two orders conflict, they quietly count seconds in a way they remember from home. A recognizable detail can suggest a connection without settling it. If freed, their opinion of the player develops from what actually happened. The attack site, opposing army, war objective, paladin levels and rival name remain proposals or open decisions.

The older blueprint’s Vladimir Kujo name gives way to the new proposed full name and short form for this pitch. Older biography details such as immortality need explicit reconciliation. Vladimir is a former Ardelis court mage who left long ago. His power belongs in the company of a great Glowhaven founder and is seldom recognized. Reveal that power through a precise intervention he has reason to make, and let the player own the pivotal choice.

## A place to live between chapters

Keep Brookmeadow as the player’s familiar place and the nearby outer port as the place where a small errand can become a day out. Three useful repeat contacts are a quay tally clerk who hires for short shifts, a mender who buys damaged nets and sells their own repaired stock, and an innkeeper with an occasional music night. A flour run from Haruho’s mill to a waterside cook can be one short optional job. Kenta’s effort to earn a stage spot can persist as its own relationship. The fishing and coastal trade already in the project give these scenes ordinary business. Treat the new port as distinct from **Whitecrest Harbor**, and wait for the author’s placed map before selecting a name, key, pins or travel addresses.

For general lore, lead with things people can know and use in play. Expand Glowhaven and Ardelis’s diplomatic relationship, competing views within Ardelis, the limits of ordinary summoning and the working relationship between Brookmeadow and the coast. Preserve Glowhaven’s independent status and existing national lore. Put the exceptional ritual’s provenance and the command method in the relevant private quest and NPC material. Everyday summoners can still teach their ordinary discipline.

## A hundred more scrolls

Target roughly 100 additions. A first allocation for review is **30 practical and trade**, **25 defense and rescue**, **25 combat**, **15 research and specialist movement**, and **5 rare discoveries**. Give each a plausible source such as mill and harbor work, school exercises, specialists, field discoveries and commissions. The Shadowspire route may offer a small number of rare working formulas. Keep the reincarnation diagram itself a piece of evidence so finding it cannot turn it into a learnable mass-summoning spell. General scroll availability should support the other starts too.

Prototype real teaching scrolls with compact item descriptions. The established workflow checks actual possession and successful study, grants the learned Formula trait and exact ability to the reader, and consumes one copy. Failure, duplicates and other players’ inventories need the same coverage as the current set. Keep costs and school foundations consistent with the existing system. A short proving batch should cover mundane utility, defensive timing and one rare formula before scaling the catalogue.

The current compact item section is **209,966 of 250,000 UTF-16 units**. A hundred items get roughly **400 units each including their keys**, before reserving headroom. Existing teaching-scroll records average about 1,155 units before keys. Keep extended reading text out of the new records unless space is measured for it. The world has **390 root definitions with semantic conditions**. Another hundred on the existing per-formula pattern would take that count to 490 before Rebirth’s story gates and other runtime checks. Compare compact, mechanically gated formulas against a smaller first import. Check both Studio collection limits and per-turn eligibility when a character carries many scrolls. ## What the reference worlds actually do

The locally supplied **sources/MushokuTensei.json** has 42 starts and 184 root triggers. Its default party has 77 hidden quests, 72 quest triggers, 10 arcs and 14 narrative events. **Overlord.json** is the available second reference, used on the assumption that “Overworld” may refer to it. It has 66 hidden quests and six events at the root, with no root trigger definitions in the supplied export. These findings describe authored configuration. Mushoku Tensei shows how household scenes and late turning points can belong to the same long story. Its timeline-specific starts and fixed outcomes fit a retelling of known events. Our single featured start leads into one continuous save. A small set of native quest handoffs, bounded scene events and persistent outcomes should carry that save through detours without requiring alternate chapter starts or the reference’s per-objective triggers.
