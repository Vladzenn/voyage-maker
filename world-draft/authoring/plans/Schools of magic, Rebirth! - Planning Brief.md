# Schools of magic, Rebirth!

## The Wizard of Shadowspire

Planning brief · 4 November 2026

**Release scope.** Feature the new reincarnated opening first in Story Starts and in the announcement. Build the Wizard of Shadowspire as its long-form storyline. Expand the general lore and target about 100 additional scrolls. The author is adding a trading port outside Glowhaven beside Brookmeadow. It gives the player somewhere to work, shop and spend time between chapters. This pass drafts the content and leaves it unapplied to WorldAlpha.

The two author prompts are recorded verbatim in [Author proposals](#author-proposals) below.

## The featured start

**Proposed menu title** · Rebirth — A Morning in Brookmeadow

**Menu description** · You wake up in a body that isn’t yours, with a whole other life still in your memory. The people who found you unconscious on the shore have brought you to Brookmeadow. There’s a bed, a hot breakfast, and a neighbor who’s late for work. For now, that’s enough.

> You wake up in a body that isn’t yours.
>
> The voice that comes out when you clear your throat is wrong. There’s a basin on the stool beside the bed. The face in the water moves when you do. You still remember the room where you last went to sleep, down to the view from its window.
>
> “If you can sit up, there’s broth.”
>
> The woman at the stove sets a bowl on the table. A rag is folded under its rim to stop it rocking.
>
> “We found you unconscious on the shore. Kenta thought you were dead. He’s been out there practicing how to apologize in case you heard him.”
>
> From outside comes a short, indignant “I was checking if they could hear me.”
>
> She lifts the latch. In the yard, Kenta Matsuba is sharing a pear with a cart horse. Farther down the lane a mill wheel turns. A sliver of blue light travels down the iron hinge of the stove, and the woman nudges it back into place with a poker.
>
> “You can have breakfast before you tell me what to call you,” she says. “Or the other way round.”

The opening ends before the player answers. The bowl and the offered name give them something immediate to respond to. They can ask where they are, test the new body, explain or conceal their memories, or walk out to meet Kenta. The narration should adapt the bodily cues to the chosen character. The last remembered room in the sample is a replaceable detail. The player owns their name, ancestry, appearance, past-life particulars and response. Keep the previous occupant’s fate open until the author decides what happened.

**Opening circle.** Stage the recovery in a cottage off **Brookmeadow - Mill Lane**. This is a proposed recovery scene within an existing area. Keep the exact shore address open until the author places the port and confirms the rescue site. Use one new recurring cottage resident as the practical caregiver. She wants her spare room back before the next market day and has a batch of lamp covers to finish. Kenta already works around **Brookmeadow - South Road**. His habit of making promises on the spot and his desire for a paid music booking give him reasons to check in and reasons to leave. **Haruho Taneda** already works on Mill Lane and can be the first recurring work contact. Bring him in after the player steps outside or asks about work. **Shiori Kawase** can reappear later at **Pinewater River - Brookmeadow Ford**, where she has her own work and judgment. Establish why each person is present. Keep help, friendship, lodging arrangements, lessons and recruitment voluntary.

**Summoned one** · “You woke in another body with the memories of your previous life intact. So far, you alone know what happened to you.”

Use this as a narrative origin with no proposed stat package, automatic magical mastery or public allegiance. Story-start trait eligibility controls which choices are shown. Prototype a one-shot mechanical grant at tick zero, gated by the exact `story-start` and guarded against an existing trait. The start prose supplies the opening premise. Verify the grant timing and visibility in the target Studio build. Test the lobby, player sheet, public narration, NPC dialogue, quest journal and a later join. Keep the new opening separate from the existing Brookmeadow village-life and summoned-familiar starts. First in the list is the requested promotion. Whether to change the current default selection is open. Start in solo for the prototype. Shared play needs an explicit rule for who carries the origin and how the story recognizes them. ## The story across a long save

### Act I · A life in Brookmeadow

**1. The shore and the spare bed.** The player wakes in a borrowed bed. The people who carried them in say they found them unconscious on the shore. Recognition comes through the unfamiliar body and intact memories. The rescuers know where they found the player and what condition they were in. The player can decide what to tell them. Once they have had a chance to look around, a local offers two ordinary directions. There is work around town, or the player can head to the **Adventurer's Guild - Glowhaven Branch** in the main city. Introduce the branch through the normal Reception Counter and its actual registration and contract rules.

**2. Something worth coming back for.** Give the quiet stretch its own satisfying end. A suggested village episode is a market delivery on a bad river day. The mill owes a waterside cook a load of flour, Shiori closes an unsafe crossing, and Kenta has promised to arrive in time for a paid set. The player can find a safe route, renegotiate the delivery, support Kenta separately, or make other plans. A city-bound player can reach the same stage by establishing a contact, taking work or settling into guild or academy life. Allow several ordinary scenes and a meaningful foothold before the delegation enters the player's story. A level gain alone advances neither the inquiry nor the hunt.

### Act II · The Sage's visitors

**3. An inquiry without a name.** One day, an Ardelis delegation passes through and locals wonder why it is there. The delegation says it has business with the Great Sage, **Ilyra Stillglass**. A real diplomatic audience is a plausible reason to be in Glowhaven, where cooperation in Ardelis's war has already been refused. Privately, the current Ardelis court mage felt the original summoning interrupt and throw its subject off course. That supplied a broad direction. The later delegation lets the court ask about odd sightings, unusual rescues or shore traffic. Its members can compare a place and a period. They begin with no name, face or certain identification. The inquiry takes place after the player's ordinary first act.

**4. The man they recognize.** Once some of the delegation's soldiers have started asking intrusive questions, **Valkazathoth'dmir**, called **Vladimir**, interrupts in person. One officer who knew the old court recognizes him and abruptly goes quiet. The reaction shows what his name means to that officer. Vladimir asks what business with the Great Sage requires her visitors to question people on the shore. The officer backs off and the soldiers leave the encounter. Vladimir gives the player a look that could mean he has put something together, bids them farewell and goes. He offers no diagnosis, lesson, invitation or explanation in this scene. He spends most of his time at Shadowspire, where he tries to follow what Ardelis is doing. Intercepted correspondence or targeted observation could supply individual facts. His unusual excursions are authored scenes with a reason to be there.

> The officer turns. His hand comes away from the ledger.
>
> “Master Vladimir?”
>
> “The Great Sage receives visitors in the city,” Vladimir says. “What business of hers has brought you to this shore?”
>
> The officer closes the ledger and motions his people back toward the road. Vladimir watches them go. His gaze rests on you a moment longer than courtesy requires.
>
> “Good afternoon,” he says, and takes the other road.

**5. Cloaked hirelings.** The first personal attack happens later, after a real lead gives the people paying for it a place to look. A proposed lead is a shore witness or copied rescue report whose time and place match the court mage's trace, followed by a sighting that identifies the person taken in. The attackers appear to be hired people with concealed faces. The leading design is that an intermediary supplied advanced magitech constructs that pass for people at a glance. They try to seize the target or test their defenses. If genuinely captured alive, the constructs automatically go inert and are treated as dead. A capture attempt, an offer of surrender or a momentary knockdown is insufficient. An inspected shell can yield a clue such as a fused regulator, a workshop stamp or a broken command component. Give a player who kills the attackers, escapes them or sees none of them a separate route to evidence. These attacks are exclusive to the Rebirth opening and its **Summoned one** bearer. A small, finite sequence is enough.

**6. An invitation to Shadowspire.** In a later eligible attack, a one-time chance may bring Vladimir into the scene if he has a plausible reason to be close. He can block a second attacker, offer a way out, or interrupt a seizure in progress. The player's choices and achieved outcomes still stand. He can then offer a meeting. If he misses the attack, a one-time invitation can arrive through an established channel once the player has recovered evidence or an investigator has made contact. That route also works if an ambush never happens. Accepting the invitation leads to **Shadowspire - Outer Clearing** for the prototype. The tower's sealed threshold can remain in place while this scene is proved.

At Shadowspire he finally explains that Ardelis used his old diagram to summon a hero for its war, and why he thinks the first casting went astray. He can bring evidence from his surveillance. The player owns disclosure of their memories and can ask for help, take the evidence to Glowhaven, or walk away. The last question is what to do with the discovery that the court is preparing another summoning. **Act III starts when that preparation becomes an actionable threat.** A refusal of Vladimir's invitation leaves an evidence route through a civic or scholarly contact.

### Act III · The weapon they wanted

**7. Stop the next summoning program.** Give the player an achievable objective against Ardelis. A proposed first operation is to find a relay, operator, or record that ties a new field trial to the court. The player can disable part of the apparatus, warn Glowhaven, protect people the court is targeting, or secure proof for the Great Sage. Which route they choose affects the resources, civilian exposure and evidence in the field test. Ardelis can make the breakthrough independently, including in a run where it never identifies or captures the player. A later, level-appropriate confrontation with actual **Ardelis paladins** remains an optional escalation if they have a credible personal lead. Those paladins are people who want the player alive for soul study. Preserve fight, evasion, surrender, custody, escape and negotiated release as distinct outcomes. Any actual extraction would be a further consequence of play.

**8. The second arrival.** Ardelis successfully repeats the ritual and adds command magic. It sends a stronger, controlled woman into Glowhaven as a weapons test. Her behavior under orders is neutral and eerily mechanical. A proposed scene opens near a city gate. She has orders to keep a breach open long enough to measure the city's response, and the fighting spills into a street used by civilians. The player can evacuate people, disrupt a command component, confront her or gather evidence. A late arrival can find witnesses and damage from an attack recorded in that save. The scene establishes the crisis and leaves its resolution in play.

**9. The choice that was yours.** If she survives and breaks free, the player can ask what she wants to be called, echoing the question from the cottage. She may refuse help, seek shelter, leave, or become a recurring rival with her own reasons to act. If control persists, the player can pursue its source. If she dies, witnesses and recovered evidence can still carry the political resolution. Resolve this deployment and the local part of the program. The larger war leaves room for later stories.

He left the Ardelis court long ago and is comparable in power to a great Glowhaven founder. His wider reputation remains obscure. Older details such as immortality and his original reason for drawing the diagram need explicit reconciliation. The player's previous body, exact war objective and the precise interruption are open.

Their restricted behavior, finite range and real parts give their disguise and shutdown a common explanation. ## A place to live between chapters

Keep Brookmeadow as the player’s familiar place and the nearby outer port as the place where a small errand can become a day out. Three useful repeat contacts are a quay tally clerk who hires for short shifts, a mender who buys damaged nets and sells their own repaired stock, and an innkeeper with an occasional music night. A flour run from Haruho’s mill to a waterside cook can be one short optional job. Kenta’s effort to earn a stage spot can persist as its own relationship. The fishing and coastal trade already in the project give these scenes ordinary business. Treat the new port as distinct from **Whitecrest Harbor**, and wait for the author’s placed map before selecting a name, key, pins or travel addresses.

For general lore, lead with things people can know and use in play. Expand Glowhaven and Ardelis’s diplomatic relationship, competing views within Ardelis, the limits of ordinary summoning and the working relationship between Brookmeadow and the coast. Preserve Glowhaven’s independent status and existing national lore. Put the exceptional ritual’s provenance and the command method in the relevant private quest and NPC material. Everyday summoners can still teach their ordinary discipline.

## A hundred more scrolls

Target roughly 100 additions. A first allocation for review is **30 practical and trade**, **25 defense and rescue**, **25 combat**, **15 research and specialist movement**, and **5 rare discoveries**. Give each a plausible source such as mill and harbor work, school exercises, specialists, field discoveries and commissions. The Shadowspire route may offer a small number of rare working formulas. Keep the reincarnation diagram itself a piece of evidence so finding it cannot turn it into a learnable mass-summoning spell. General scroll availability should support the other starts too.

Prototype real teaching scrolls with compact item descriptions. The established workflow checks actual possession and successful study, grants the learned Formula trait and exact ability to the reader, and consumes one copy. Failure, duplicates and other players’ inventories need the same coverage as the current set. Keep costs and school foundations consistent with the existing system. A short proving batch should cover mundane utility, defensive timing and one rare formula before scaling the catalogue.

The current compact item section is **209,966 of 250,000 UTF-16 units**. A hundred items get roughly **400 units each including their keys**, before reserving headroom. Existing teaching-scroll records average about 1,155 units before keys. Keep extended reading text out of the new records unless space is measured for it. The world has **390 root definitions with semantic conditions**. Another hundred on the existing per-formula pattern would take that count to 490 before Rebirth’s story gates and other runtime checks. Compare compact, mechanically gated formulas against a smaller first import. Check both Studio collection limits and per-turn eligibility when a character carries many scrolls. ## Possible failure cases

| Failure case | How to handle it |
| --- | --- |
| **The origin is missing or appears on the wrong character.** A tick-zero grant is skipped, a later join inherits it, or another player's trait makes a party-level gate pass. | Verify the exact start and the eligible player before any personal attack. Keep the first prototype solo. If ownership is ambiguous, suspend the ambush in that save and let the public delegation scene work on its own. Test a fresh run, a reload, a later join and a different start. |
| **The story exposes the secret.** The journal, generated NPC dialogue or public scene treats `Summoned one` as a name Ardelis already knows. | Keep player-facing and narrator-only knowledge separate. Gate individual suspicions on an observed or reported lead. Check who actually heard a confession before propagating it. A leak into NPC behavior blocks release of the opening. |
| **The hunt arrives early, or the story stalls when the player leaves Brookmeadow.** A high level substitutes for elapsed story, or a village quest becomes the sole prerequisite. | Gate the delegation on established ordinary play and allow a guild or city contact as an alternate entry. Gate the personal attack later on a credible lead. Test fast leveling, immediate departure and a false witness report. |
| **Vladimir appears at the wrong moment or acts like an assigned mentor.** He steps into a scene he cannot reach, reveals the secret during the first encounter or interrupts a fight the player has already resolved. | Require actual scene eligibility and availability. Keep the delegation intervention brief. A missed appearance moves the offer to the established message or contact route. Validate that leaving, losing or winning a fight stays resolved when the invitation appears. |
| **The encounter rerolls or repeats.** Save reloads, repeated dialogue or lingering in an area trigger another attack, intervention or invitation. | Record the encounter and its single roll under the same stable identity. Keep the invitation and its acceptance or refusal under one owner. The fallback can offer a route to the evidence after refusal, with no automatic acceptance or travel. |
| **The constructs shut down for the wrong reason, or fail to shut down at all.** The judge treats a threat, a knockout, a brief hold or capture of the player as a secured construct. The typed effect may also fail to apply or run twice. | Scope the check to a living, named construct in the actual encounter. Confirm the observed capture, then verify the native Health outcome before recording a confirmed shutdown. Test repeat callbacks and reload between those steps. If semantic recognition is unreliable, use an explicit restraint objective. Keep the first build to one verified construct until the full transition works. |
| **The clue disappears or duplicates.** A corpse is removed, a construct escapes, or the player finds a part through two routes. | Allow corroboration through an independent report or workshop record. Award progress through one quest owner. Confirm actual inspection or receipt before granting an inventory item. Track the inference once even if several pieces of evidence support it. |
| **The second trial assumes an outcome that never happened.** The script expects the player to have been captured, claims a city attack occurred offstage with no save record, or brings back a dead rival. | Let the court reach the breakthrough independently. Store whether the field test actually started and how it resolved. Aftermath requires a recorded attack. Use the rival's observed survival and freedom status to decide whether a later meeting is available. |
| **The expanded story competes with existing triggers.** Carrying scrolls, crossing busy scenes or taking several quests causes the Rebirth check to crowd out normal play. | Keep gates local, with one semantic judgment for the event currently in play where possible. Check worst-case overlap with the existing trigger budget before adding a second ambush. Defer extra encounters if the first scene cannot fit reliably. |

Mushoku Tensei shows how household scenes and late turning points can belong to the same long story. Its timeline-specific starts and fixed outcomes fit a retelling of known events. Our single featured start leads into one continuous save. A small set of native quest handoffs, bounded scene events and persistent outcomes should carry that save through detours without requiring alternate chapter starts or the reference’s per-objective triggers.
