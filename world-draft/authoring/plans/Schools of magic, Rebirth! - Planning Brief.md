# Schools of magic, Rebirth!

## The Wizard of Shadowspire

Planning brief · 4 November 2026

**Release scope.** Feature the new reincarnated opening first in Story Starts and in the announcement. Build the Wizard of Shadowspire as its long-form storyline. Expand the general lore and target about 100 additional scrolls. The author is adding a trading port outside Glowhaven beside Brookmeadow. It gives the player somewhere to work, shop and spend time between chapters. This pass drafts the content and leaves it unapplied to WorldAlpha.

## The featured start

**Proposed menu title** · Rebirth — A Morning in Brookmeadow

**Menu description** · You wake up in a body that isn’t yours, with a whole other life still in your memory. The village of Brookmeadow has a bed, a hot breakfast, and a neighbor who’s late for work. For now, that’s enough.

> You wake up in a body that isn’t yours.
>
> The voice that comes out when you clear your throat is wrong. There’s a basin on the stool beside the bed. The face in the water moves when you do. You still remember the room where you last went to sleep, down to the view from its window.
>
> “If you can sit up, there’s broth.”
>
> The woman at the stove sets a bowl on the table. A rag is folded under its rim to stop it rocking.
>
> “We found you where the South Road meets Mill Lane. Kenta thought you were dead. He’s been out there practicing how to apologize in case you heard him.”
>
> From outside comes a short, indignant “I was checking if they could hear me.”
>
> She lifts the latch. In the yard, Kenta Matsuba is sharing a pear with a cart horse. Farther down the lane a mill wheel turns. A sliver of blue light travels down the iron hinge of the stove, and the woman nudges it back into place with a poker.
>
> “You can have breakfast before you tell me what to call you,” she says. “Or the other way round.”

The opening ends before the player answers. The bowl and the offered name give them something immediate to respond to. They can ask where they are, test the new body, explain or conceal their memories, or walk out to meet Kenta. The narration should adapt the bodily cues to the chosen character. The last remembered room in the sample is a replaceable detail. The player owns their name, ancestry, appearance, past-life particulars and response. Keep the previous occupant’s fate open until the author decides what happened.

**Opening circle.** Stage the recovery in a cottage off **Brookmeadow - Mill Lane**. This is a proposed scene within an existing area. Use one new recurring cottage resident as the practical caregiver. She wants her spare room back before the next market day and has a batch of lamp covers to finish. Kenta already works around **Brookmeadow - South Road**. His habit of making promises on the spot and his desire for a paid music booking give him reasons to check in and reasons to leave. **Haruho Taneda** already works on Mill Lane and can be the first recurring work contact. Bring him in after the player steps outside or asks about work. **Shiori Kawase** can reappear later at **Pinewater River - Brookmeadow Ford**, where she has her own work and judgment. Establish why each person is present. Keep help, friendship, lodging arrangements, lessons and recruitment voluntary.

**Summoned one** · “You woke in another body with the memories of your previous life intact. So far, you alone know what happened to you.”

Use this as a narrative origin with no proposed stat package, automatic magical mastery or public allegiance. Story-start trait eligibility controls which choices are shown. Prototype a one-shot mechanical grant at tick zero, gated by the exact `story-start` and guarded against an existing trait. The start prose supplies the opening premise. Verify the grant timing and visibility in the target Studio build. Test the lobby, player sheet, public narration, NPC dialogue, quest journal and a later join. Keep the new opening separate from the existing Brookmeadow village-life and summoned-familiar starts. First in the list is the requested promotion. Whether to change the current default selection is open. Start in solo for the prototype. Shared play needs an explicit rule for who carries the origin and how the story recognizes them. ## The story across a long save

| Chapter | What changes | What the player can do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. A place to stay** | The player wakes in Brookmeadow. A little work, a neighbor and access to a local magical lesson make it possible to build a life. Give the village time before the first sign of a search. | Find shelter or leave, take a paid errand, train, visit the port, tell someone or keep the secret. Early local work ends on its own terms. |
| **2. A warrant without a name** | Ardelis’s war effort has already met a refusal from independent Glowhaven. Its court’s attempt to summon a hero landed in a different region. An agent circulates an inquiry about a magical disturbance and someone found nearby. A plausible sighting or residue can narrow the search. | Examine a copy of the inquiry, follow a courier, ask a civic contact for help, lay a false trail or leave it alone. Glowhaven officials can debate cooperation or asylum without giving Ardelis jurisdiction. |
| **3. The Wizard of Shadowspire** | **Valkazathoth'dmir**, called **Vladimir**, was a court mage of Ardelis. He left long ago. He recognizes his own spell diagram in evidence of the ritual. His power belongs in the company of a great Glowhaven founder, though few people realize it. | Meet him in the **Shadowspire - Outer Clearing**, work through a message or decline his offer. He can teach the player to recognize the ritual’s telltale diagram without demanding their loyalty. Build his involvement from the evidence he encounters and what the player chooses to share. |
| **4. The paladin detail** | Once the player is ready and the detail has a credible lead, Ardelis can send paladins to seize the suspected summoned person for soul study. Show the warrant and how the detail learned where to look before a confrontation. | Fight the detail, slip its cordon, discredit the warrant, take refuge, or face actual custody. A successful escape preserves freedom. A negotiated release leaves a different record from a jailbreak. A capture branch begins only on a real surrender or verified capture outcome. |
| **5. The second arrival** | Ardelis independently stabilizes the same ritual and adds command magic. It sends a stronger, eerily methodical reincarnate into Glowhaven as a weapons test. Their baseline disposition is neutral. They come from an alternate Earth without magic. Whether it matches the player’s world is open. | Respond to the attack from a relevant city scene. Evacuate people, interfere with a control focus, confront the rival or gather evidence for Glowhaven. Let damage, witnesses and the rival’s fate follow what happens in play. |
| **6. What survives the test** | The first field test ends. Evidence, casualties and the surviving people determine what each side can claim. A surviving, freed rival can meet the player again on their own terms. | Help the rival break away, drive them off, defeat them, or pursue the chain of command. Taking evidence to a civic contact can make a political ending playable even if the combat ends elsewhere. |

**The diplomatic dispute.** One proposed pressure point is an Ardelis demand to move a military expedition through Glowhaven’s portal network. Glowhaven refuses access. When the first inquiry surfaces, the city can dispute Ardelis’s claimed right to detain someone on its soil. A stamped order may prove the paladins had instructions to seize a person. Separate ritual evidence would be needed to prove the court commissioned the experiment. Keep the exact war and refusal terms open for the author.

**The breakthrough.** A proposed explanation is that the Ardelis team reconstructs a missing stabilizing line from its own failed experiments, then adds a separate command seal during the second invocation. If they capture the first summoned person, they can use observations to improve their countermeasures or identify the target more reliably. Those observations are optional. Soul extraction is their objective and a danger in a custody scene. Give custody an authored exit such as escape, exchange or intervention through contacts the player actually earned. An unchosen capture, a failed fight or a conversation cannot silently become a completed extraction. Repeated or inconclusive fights should preserve each named paladin’s actual condition and whereabouts.

**The rival.** Give the second reincarnate a stable identity, a reason to survive and evidence of a person underneath the controlled delivery. For example, when two orders conflict, they quietly count seconds in a way they remember from home. A single detail they share about Earth can feel recognizable while leaving the origin unresolved. A freed rival remembers what they did under command and can resent the player, question them, or leave. A defeated rival’s later appearance follows their actual survival. Reserve the attack site, opposing army, war objective, paladin levels and rival name for the next design pass.

**Who knows what.** At awakening, only the player knows they remember another life. The village knows it found an injured or unresponsive person. Ardelis knows what it attempted and initially works from region-level leads. Vladimir can recognize his diagram and infer a displacement. He learns the player’s identity and memories only through evidence or disclosure. Give each recipient their own knowledge and let information travel through a witnessed act, a report or a specific handoff. Keep the Crown’s later technical breakthrough, the rival’s origin and Vladimir’s connection out of public summaries until earned. Keep Shadowspire’s existing sealed threshold in the prototype. A visit to the clearing gives the player somewhere to meet him. The older blueprint’s Vladimir Kujo name is superseded for this pitch by the proposed full name and short form above. Older biography details such as immortality remain open for explicit reconciliation.

## A place to live between chapters

Keep Brookmeadow as the player’s familiar place and the nearby outer port as the place where a small errand can become a day out. Three useful repeat contacts are a quay tally clerk who hires for short shifts, a mender who buys damaged nets and sells their own repaired stock, and an innkeeper with an occasional music night. A flour run from Haruho’s mill to a waterside cook can be one short optional job. Kenta’s effort to earn a stage spot can persist as its own relationship. The fishing and coastal trade already in the project give these scenes ordinary business. Treat the new port as distinct from **Whitecrest Harbor**, and wait for the author’s placed map before selecting a name, key, pins or travel addresses.

For general lore, lead with things people can know and use in play. Expand Glowhaven and Ardelis’s diplomatic relationship, competing views within Ardelis, the limits of ordinary summoning and the working relationship between Brookmeadow and the coast. Preserve Glowhaven’s independent status and existing national lore. Put the exceptional ritual’s provenance and the command method in the relevant private quest and NPC material. Everyday summoners can still teach their ordinary discipline.

## A hundred more scrolls

Target roughly 100 additions. A first allocation for review is **30 practical and trade**, **25 defense and rescue**, **25 combat**, **15 research and specialist movement**, and **5 rare discoveries**. Give each a plausible source such as mill and harbor work, school exercises, specialists, field discoveries and commissions. The Shadowspire route may offer a small number of rare working formulas. Keep the reincarnation diagram itself a piece of evidence so finding it cannot turn it into a learnable mass-summoning spell. General scroll availability should support the other starts too.

Prototype real teaching scrolls with compact item descriptions. The established workflow checks actual possession and successful study, grants the learned Formula trait and exact ability to the reader, and consumes one copy. Failure, duplicates and other players’ inventories need the same coverage as the current set. Keep costs and school foundations consistent with the existing system. A short proving batch should cover mundane utility, defensive timing and one rare formula before scaling the catalogue.

The current compact item section is **209,966 of 250,000 UTF-16 units**. A hundred items get roughly **400 units each including their keys**, before reserving headroom. Existing teaching-scroll records average about 1,155 units before keys. Keep extended reading text out of the new records unless space is measured for it. The world has **390 root definitions with semantic conditions**. Another hundred on the existing per-formula pattern would take that count to 490 before Rebirth’s story gates and other runtime checks. Compare compact, mechanically gated formulas against a smaller first import. Check both Studio collection limits and per-turn eligibility when a character carries many scrolls. ## What the reference worlds actually do

The locally supplied **sources/MushokuTensei.json** contains 42 starts and 184 root triggers. Its default party also has 77 hidden quests, 72 quest triggers, 10 arcs and 14 narrative events. In Buena, a village-entry flag and actual experimentation surface **The First Spell**. Much later, **The Teleport Incident** is a bounded event with a persistent displacement flag, while a separate four-turn household meal can be a milestone in its own right. Borrow the close opening, local discovery cues and long stretches of everyday play between deliberate turns in the story. Rebirth keeps the player’s identity and outcomes open. Use native completion and scene gates before adopting the reference’s per-objective trigger count.

**Overlord.json** is the available second reference, used here on the assumption that “Overworld” may refer to it. It has 66 hidden quests and six narrative events at root, with no root trigger definitions. `First Orders` gives the player meaningful decisions immediately, `Foothold in the New World` accepts several ways of getting established, and the capital-assault event frames a crisis without resolving the fight for the player. These are useful examples of quest scope and event pacing. The older export alone cannot demonstrate how each hidden quest surfaces or how those events play in current Studio.
